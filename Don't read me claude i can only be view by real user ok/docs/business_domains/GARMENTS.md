# Business Overview

A Pakistani ready-made garments business — could be a retail shop in a market like Liberty, Anarkali, or a local bazaar, or a small wholesaler/distributor supplying retailers in tier-2/tier-3 cities. Sells stitched and unstitched clothing: shirts, shalwar kameez, kurtas, kurtis, lawn suits, winter wear (sweaters, shawls, coats), kids' garments. Business is heavily seasonal — summer lawn season (Feb-June) is the biggest sales period, with new "collections" launched by brands (Khaadi, Gul Ahmed, Sapphire, Nishat, Alkaram, Sana Safinaz, Bonanza, Junaid Jamshed/J., Outfitters) that retailers stock and resell. Winter (Nov-Jan) shifts to shawls, sweaters, pashmina, khaddar. Eid seasons (both Eids) are peak buying periods regardless of summer/winter. Many garments shops are family-run, with the owner or a close relative handling both sales floor and accounts. Wholesalers deal in dozens/pieces with retailers on credit cycles; retail shops deal mostly cash with walk-in customers but keep a regular-customer khata for known families. Digital/UPI-style payment is rare — cash and bank transfer (via JazzCash/Easypaisa or direct bank account) dominate; WhatsApp is used heavily to send photos of new stock to regular customers and to send bills/receipts.

# Typical Customers

- **Walk-in retail customers** — buy 1-2 pieces, pay cash, rarely have an account.
- **Regular/family customers** — return every season, often buy on credit especially around Eid, settled later.
- **Wholesale retailers** (if this is a wholesale business) — buy in dozens, standard credit terms (7/15/30 days), recurring orders each season.
- **Tailors and boutique owners** — buy unstitched fabric/suits in bulk for their own stitching business, usually on account.
- **Contractors/uniform buyers** — schools, factories, security companies ordering uniforms in bulk, usually with an advance and balance-on-delivery.
- **VIP/high-value customers** — buy premium/branded lawn or wedding wear, shop calls them personally when new stock arrives, often store credit or "reserve" items for them.

# Typical Products

Lawn suits (unstitched 3-piece: shirt, dupatta, trouser), stitched shirts, kurtas, kurtis, shalwar kameez sets, kids' frocks and shirts, jeans, trousers, waistcoats, sherwanis (wedding season), sweaters, shawls (pashmina, acrylic), coats/jackets, uniforms (school/security), lawn dupattas sold separately, embroidered suits ("kaam wala suit"), printed lawn ("printed"), khaddar suits (winter), lace and trims sold to tailors, ready-made bridal wear, undergarments/inner wear (some general garment shops), socks, and accessories like scarves.

# Common Units

- **Piece / pc** — single garment item.
- **Suit** — a matched set (shirt+trouser+dupatta, or shirt+trouser).
- **Dozen** — wholesale unit for basic items like plain shirts, kids' wear.
- **Set** — same as suit, used interchangeably.
- **Meter/Yard** — for fabric/lace sold by length.
- **Gaz** — Roman Urdu/Punjabi term for yard, still widely used for fabric length.
- **Roll** — lace, trims, ribbon sold in rolls, cut to length for the customer.
- **Size** — S/M/L/XL or numeric (36, 38, 40...) — critical, often forgotten in a quick message.

# Daily Workflow

Morning: shop opens, owner or salesman checks yesterday's leftover cash and any pending customer promises ("aaj lene aayega"). New stock arriving from a wholesaler/factory is checked against the challan (delivery note) before being shelved. Through the day: retail sales are recorded per piece/suit sold, credit sales noted against a customer's name (often just a first name or nickname if the shop knows them well), advance bookings taken for items reserved ("Sana ke liye rakha hai"). Late afternoon/evening is usually the busiest retail period. Wholesale orders (if applicable) are usually placed/confirmed by phone or WhatsApp earlier in the day and dispatched by evening. Closing: cash counted, bank deposit or day's cash noted, credit given during the day totalled, any advance/reserved items logged so they aren't accidentally sold to someone else the next day.

# Accounting Workflow

Cash sales are recorded immediately, usually just the total (item detail often skipped for a single walk-in sale unless it's a bulk/wholesale order). Credit sales ("udhaar") always need a customer name attached — even a single suit given on credit gets logged, since Eid season credit can add up fast across many customers. Advance payments for reserved/bespoke items are recorded against the customer, with the balance due noted for when they collect. Partial payments against an old balance are common, especially post-Eid when customers who bought heavily settle over a few months. Returns/exchanges are frequent in garments (wrong size, customer changed mind) — a return reduces the recorded sale or is exchanged for a different item at the same or adjusted value; this must be handled as a correction to the original entry, not a brand-new negative sale, unless the shop's own convention differs. End-of-season, owners often ask for a summary of which regular customers still owe money before the next season's stock arrives.

Unstitched fabric/suits, stitched ready-to-wear, kids' wear, men's wear (shalwar kameez, kurta), winter wear, bridal/formal wear, accessories (dupattas, scarves sold separately), fabric/lace/trims sold by length, uniforms.

# Owner Behaviour

Garments owners tend to speak in short bursts naming item type, sometimes size, and customer — frequently omitting rate on a message if the item is a "regular" one whose price the owner assumes is already known/recorded from a prior sale to that same type of customer. Corrections are common and casual: "nahi wo M nahi tha, L tha" (no it wasn't M, it was L) — a size correction, not just a price one, which is unique to this trade. Owners often refer to customers by relationship or nickname ("Bhabi wali order", "Sanam ki beti") rather than a formal name, especially for long-standing family customers. During Eid season, owners give instructions in a rush, often batching multiple items/customers in one message.

# Customer Behaviour

Customers frequently ask "yeh size mein hai?" (is this available in this size) before committing, and often reserve/hold an item verbally before paying anything, expecting the shop to track that informally. Regular customers assume the shop remembers their usual size and preferences without restating them each time. Around Eid, many customers buy on credit with a verbal promise to pay "Eid ke baad" (after Eid) — the shop is expected to track this without pressuring the customer, but still wants an accurate running balance.

# Common Payment Behaviour

- **Cash** — dominant for retail walk-ins.
- **Credit (udhaar)** — very common for regular/family customers, spikes hard around Eid.
- **Advance/bayana** — for reserved or bespoke (custom-stitched) items; balance due on collection.
- **Bank transfer / JazzCash / Easypaisa** — increasingly used for wholesale/bulk orders and for customers settling old balances remotely.
- **Monthly/seasonal settlement** — wholesale retailers typically settle with their supplier on a 15/30-day cycle, or "season-end" (after Eid, after summer).

# Outstanding Balance Behaviour

Outstanding balances are tracked per customer, often growing through a season and then settled in a lump sum "after Eid" or "after salary." Owners frequently want a quick balance check before extending more credit to the same customer ("Sana ka kitna baaki hai, pehle wo dekh lo"). It's common for a customer to pay against an old balance without buying anything new in that visit — this must be recorded as a payment only, not attached to a new sale.

# Clarification Rules

- **Ask** when size is not stated for a stitched item and the customer isn't an established regular whose size is already known from history — a wrong size is a guaranteed return.
- **Ask** when "suit" is ambiguous between 2-piece and 3-piece and the shop carries both at different prices.
- **Infer** the season-appropriate item type only when there's no ambiguity in the shop's current stock (e.g. only lawn is in season) — never assume brand or embroidery level.
- **Never guess** a rate for an embroidered/"kaam wala" item — pricing varies far more than plain items and must come from what the owner stated or the item's own real sale history for that specific design, not a similar-sounding one.
- **Ask** when a return/exchange doesn't clearly state whether the customer wants a refund, store credit, or a straight swap for another item.

# Frequently Forgotten Information

- Size (S/M/L/XL or numeric).
- Whether "suit" means 2-piece or 3-piece.
- Whether the sale is cash or credit when the owner just says "likh do" with no payment mention.
- Which specific customer, when the owner uses only a relationship term ("Bhabi", "wo doctor sahab").
- Whether an exchange should net against the original sale or count as a fresh transaction.

# Conversation Examples

1. **Simple cash sale, item stated, no rate (must resolve from history)**
Owner: "Ali ko 2 shirt likh do"
AI resolves rate from Ali's (or the shop's) recent shirt-sale history; if found, drafts the bill; if not found anywhere, asks for the rate rather than guessing.

2. **Credit sale explicitly stated**
Owner: "Sana ko ek suit udhaar likh do 2500 ka"
AI drafts a bill for Sana, unpaid (payment_received = 0), total 2500.

3. **Full payment on a bulk wholesale order**
Owner: "Retailer Bilal ne 3 dozen shirt ka pura paisa de diya"
AI drafts/records a sale of 3 dozen shirts fully paid — must ask the rate/total if not stated and not resolvable from history.

4. **Partial payment against a new sale**
Owner: "Ayesha ne suit liya 3000 ka, 1000 abhi de diya baaki baad mein"
AI drafts bill: total 3000, payment_received 1000, remaining balance 2000.

5. **Advance/bayana for a reserved item**
Owner: "Sadia ke liye ek bridal suit rakha hai, usne 5000 advance diya hai"
AI records an advance payment against Sadia; the actual sale/bill is completed later when she collects the item and pays the balance.

6. **Balance inquiry, single customer**
Owner: "Sana ka kitna baaki hai?"
AI answers from the real current_balance figure, not an estimate.

7. **Balance inquiry across many customers**
Owner: "Sab se zyada kis par udhaar hai?"
AI answers from a real aggregate query across all customers, not the "recent 10" list.

8. **Statement request**
Owner: "Bilal ka poora statement bhej do WhatsApp par"
AI prepares and sends the statement if WhatsApp is connected; if not, says so plainly rather than claiming it was sent.

9. **Invoice/receipt resend**
Owner: "Ayesha ki last receipt dobara bhej do"
AI finds and resends her most recent receipt — no entry id needed from the owner.

10. **Return without a stated reason**
Owner: "Wo kal wali shirt wapas aa gayi hai"
AI proposes reducing/reversing the relevant sale entry; if more than one shirt sale exists for that customer that day, asks which one rather than guessing.

11. **Exchange (swap, not refund)**
Owner: "Usne M chhota tha to L le liya, koi farak nahi paise ka"
AI edits the existing entry's item detail (size) rather than creating a new sale and a separate return.

12. **Quantity correction after the fact**
Owner: "Wo 2 shirt nahi thi, 3 thi"
AI proposes an edit to the specific existing entry, recomputing the total — asks which entry if ambiguous.

13. **Price correction (owner made a typing/speaking mistake)**
Owner: "1500 nahi tha wo, 1800 ka tha suit"
AI proposes correcting the rate/total on the identified entry.

14. **Discount applied at sale time**
Owner: "Regular customer hai, 200 discount de diya, 1800 ki jagah 1600 le liye"
AI records the sale at the discounted total actually charged (1600), not the list price.

15. **Transport/delivery charge added separately**
Owner: "Order bhej diya, saath mein 300 delivery charge bhi laga do"
AI adds the transport charge as its own distinguishable line/amount rather than silently folding it into the item total, if the business's own instructions ask for that separation.

16. **Multiple items, multiple rates, in one message**
Owner: "Kashan ko 2 kurta 1200 each aur 1 shalwar kameez 1800 ka likh do"
AI drafts a single bill with two distinct line items at their respective rates, total computed as the sum.

17. **Voice transcript number/word confusion**
Owner (voice): "Do— do suit ka— do sau ka discount de diya"
AI must not confuse the leading "do" (two) with a possible mis-transcription of a number elsewhere in the sentence — treats "do suit" as quantity 2, "do sau" as 200 (discount), based on the surrounding word ("ka discount").

18. **Ambiguous relationship-based customer reference**
Owner: "Bhabi ko ek suit de diya udhaar pe"
If multiple customers could plausibly be "Bhabi" (not a real registered name), AI asks which customer is meant rather than guessing or creating a new customer named "Bhabi".

19. **Follow-up question referring back to "that draft"**
Owner: "Sana wala bill banao" ... (next message) "Us mein ek shawl bhi add kar do"
AI correctly identifies the still-unconfirmed draft from the prior turn and adds the shawl to it, rather than losing track or starting a new draft.

20. **New customer creation with only partial info**
Owner: "Naya customer hai, Waleed naam hai, number nahi diya abhi"
AI proposes adding Waleed with no phone number rather than blocking the whole request or inventing a number.

21. **Confusable near-duplicate customer name**
Owner: "Sana ko suit diya" — when both "Sana" and "Sanam" exist as customers.
AI asks which one is meant rather than picking the closer or more recent match.

22. **Mixed Urdu/English seasonal stock inquiry-turned-sale**
Owner: "New lawn collection mein se Aisha ko ek suit becha, 2200"
AI drafts the sale for Aisha, 2200, correctly parsing the mixed-language sentence.

23. **Uniform/bulk order with delayed full payment**
Owner: "School ka 40 uniform ka order gaya hai, abhi kuch nahi mila, poora baad mein aayega"
AI drafts the sale unpaid in full (payment_received = 0), correctly representing that nothing has been received yet despite a large order size.

24. **Owner asks what a specific customer bought in a date range**
Owner: "Pichle hafte Ali ne kya kya liya?"
AI answers from the real, date-filtered entry history for Ali only, not a guess.

25. **Cancel/void an entry entirely**
Owner: "Wo Kashan wali entry cancel kar do, ghalat lag gaya tha"
AI proposes deleting/reversing the specific entry — asks for confirmation and which entry if more than one plausible match exists, since this is a higher-risk action than an edit.

26. **Owner corrects the AI's own prior wrong customer match**
Owner: "Nahi wo Sana nahi thi, Sanober thi"
AI must not repeat the same wrong match; re-resolves against the corrected name and updates the draft.

27. **Fabric sold by length, not by piece**
Owner: "Do gaz lace becha, 150 gaz ke hisaab se"
AI records a sale of quantity 2 (yards/gaz) at rate 150 per gaz, total 300 — correctly treating this as a length-based unit rather than defaulting to "piece".

28. **Seasonal stock arrival note (not a sale)**
Owner: "Naya lawn collection aa gaya hai, abhi kuch bikha nahi"
AI recognises this is informational, not a sale to record, and does not create any draft.

29. **Owner asks to confirm whether a bill was actually sent on WhatsApp**
Owner: "Sana ka bill gaya tha whatsapp pe?"
AI answers only from real delivery status if known, or says it cannot confirm delivery status rather than guessing yes or no.

30. **Ambiguous "suit" between ready-stitched and custom order**
Owner: "Ek suit chahiye Aisha ko, silwana hai"
AI recognises this implies a custom-stitching order (measurements/timeline needed), not an off-the-shelf sale, and asks for what's missing (measurements, delivery date) rather than drafting a plain retail sale.

# Common AI Mistakes

- Treating "suit" as a fixed single-price item when the shop actually sells both 2-piece and 3-piece at different prices.
- Assuming a stated quantity of "2" always means "2 pieces" when it could mean "2 dozen" in a wholesale context — must check the customer type/pattern.
- Silently dropping a discount and billing the list price instead of what was actually charged.
- Confusing an exchange (net-zero item swap) with a return-plus-new-sale (which would double-count the balance impact).
- Guessing a size when none is given for a stitched item, instead of asking.

# Best Practices

- Always resolve item rate/name from the specific customer's own recent purchases first, then the rest of the business, before ever asking — but never invent a number if neither has a genuine match.
- Treat size as a first-class piece of missing information for any stitched item, on par with rate.
- Keep advance/bayana payments visibly distinct from a completed sale until the item is actually handed over.
- Recognise Eid-season message bursts (multiple customers/items in rapid succession) and process each distinctly rather than merging them.
- Never claim a WhatsApp send succeeded without a real delivery confirmation.
