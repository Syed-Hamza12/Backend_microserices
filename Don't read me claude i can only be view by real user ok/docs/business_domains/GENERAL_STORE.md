# Business Overview

A Pakistani General Store sits between a small kiryana (corner grocery) and a mini-mart — a
neighborhood shop selling a broad daily-need basket: groceries, snacks, cold drinks, cigarettes,
basic stationery, household items (soap, detergent, matchbox), toiletries, and often a small
counter of mobile load/easypaisa-jazzcash agent services. It is distinct from a dedicated GROCERY
store (which focuses more narrowly on food/kitchen staples in larger quantities) in that a
General Store's basket is broader but each individual purchase is usually smaller — a customer
drops in multiple times a day for small items. It is also distinct from a Pharmacy (no medicine
beyond basic first-aid items). Most General Stores are single-owner or family-run, open long
hours (often 8am-11pm or later), and serve a fixed neighborhood clientele the owner knows by
name or by house. Stock is restocked frequently in small quantities from a nearby wholesale
market rather than bulk-imported. Cash dominates; a meaningful minority of regular households run
a monthly khata (running credit tab) settled around salary time.

# Typical Customers

- **Walk-in retail** — the overwhelming majority: a child sent to buy one item, a passerby buying
  a cold drink or cigarettes.
- **Regular/credit (khata) customers** — neighborhood households with a running monthly tab,
  settled around the 1st-10th of the month (salary cycle).
- **Small institutional buyers** — a nearby office or small school buying tea/snacks in bulk
  occasionally.
- **VIP/known customers** — long-standing families the owner extends informal, higher trust
  credit to, sometimes without writing every small purchase down immediately.

# Typical Products

Snacks: biscuits (specific brands — Sooper, Rio, Bisconni), chips (Lays, Kurkure), candy/toffee,
namkeen. Drinks: Coke/Pepsi/Sprite bottles and cans, juice boxes (Nestle, Shezan), mineral water,
energy drinks. Groceries (small-pack): atta (flour) small bags, sugar, tea (Lipton, Tapal, Vital),
ghee/cooking oil (Dalda, Habib), rice, pulses (daal) in small quantities, salt, spices in small
packets, eggs. Household: detergent (Surf Excel, Ariel), soap (Lifebuoy, Lux), matchbox, candles,
tissue paper, garbage bags. Tobacco: cigarettes (sold by pack or even single stick — "ek sutta"),
paan/gutka in some stores. Personal care: toothpaste, shampoo sachets, small-pack items sold
individually since customers often can't afford full-size. Stationery basics: pencils, notebooks,
tape. Mobile/utility: prepaid mobile load (easyload/mobicash), sometimes bill payment collection.

# Common Units

- **Piece (pcs)** — nearly everything sold individually: one biscuit packet, one soap, one
  cigarette.
- **Packet/Pack** — biscuits, chips, spices, small snack packs.
- **Kg/Gram** — atta, rice, sugar, daal, sold loose by weight in small quantities (250g, 500g,
  1kg being extremely common retail sizes).
- **Liter** — cooking oil, milk (if stocked).
- **Dozen** — eggs almost always sold/counted in dozens or half-dozens.
- **Carton/Box** — wholesale-adjacent bulk restocking unit, rarely the retail sale unit itself.
- **Single stick** — cigarettes specifically, a very common single-unit sale in this trade.

# Daily Workflow

Early morning: shop opens for the breakfast/tea-time rush — bread, milk, eggs, cigarettes.
Daytime: steady trickle of small walk-in purchases, mostly cash, rarely worth itemizing
individually in the app unless the owner chooses to. Evening: the busiest period — households
buying dinner-adjacent small groceries, children sent for snacks, the "give me on the khata" tab
requests pick up as people run short on cash before month-end. Night: cigarette/cold-drink/snack
sales continue until closing. End of month: khata settlements cluster heavily around salary
disbursement dates — owners specifically track which regular customers haven't settled yet as the
month closes.

# Accounting Workflow

The overwhelming majority of transactions are cash and are often not itemized individually in any
ledger at all — a General Store owner may only formally record the khata (credit) customers, and
treat cash sales as an aggregate daily total rather than line-by-line. Credit is given informally
and by trust, often without a receipt exchanged, purely tracked in the owner's memory/register/app.
Partial payments against a month's tab are routine ("thoda de diya, baaki mahine ke akhir mein").
Corrections are usually about a misremembered running total rather than a specific mis-keyed item,
since individual small purchases are rarely itemized to begin with.

# Common Pakistani Vocabulary

Kiryana (general daily-goods stock), khata (running monthly credit tab), udhaar/udhar (credit),
naqad (cash), maheene ka hisaab (monthly reckoning/settlement), tanqeed... (not used — skip),
girocery/kiryana items generically called "saman" (goods/stuff), "chutta" (small change — a
constant friction point: "chutta nahi hai" — no change available), "advance" for pre-payment,
"sutta"/cigarette-by-piece slang, "aata-chawal ka hisaab" (colloquial shorthand for the whole
grocery tab).

# Roman Urdu Variations

tareekh / tarikh / tareek / tarek (date); khata / khatha / khta (running tab/ledger); bhej / bejh
/ bhj (send); jama / jma (deposit/credit); likh / lkh (write/record); baaki / baki / bakaya
(remaining balance); mahina / maheena / mahine (month); hisaab / hisab (account/reckoning); pura
/ poora / pora (complete); chutta / chuta (small change); saman / smaan (goods); udhaar / udhar
(credit).

# Product Nicknames

"Ek packet" defaults contextually to whatever the customer usually buys (a regular's "usual"
biscuit or tea brand) — genuinely ambiguous without history. "Sutta"/"ek cigarette" for a single
stick sale. "Bara pack" vs "chota pack" (large vs small size) used instead of exact
gram/ml specs for common items like chips or a cold drink. "Dahi wali lassi ka packet" style
descriptive naming rather than brand name for some dairy-adjacent items. "Half dozen anda" is as
common as "poora dozen" for eggs.

# Product Categories

Snacks & Confectionery, Beverages, Grocery Staples (small-pack), Household/Cleaning, Tobacco,
Personal Care, Stationery Basics, Mobile Load/Utility Services.

# Owner Behaviour

General Store owners deal in very high transaction volume with very low individual value, so
they are terse to the point of near-telegraphic when they do bother to record something — mostly
they only speak up to the AI for khata (credit) entries and month-end settlements, since cash
sales are rarely itemized at all. They refer to regular customers by house/family reference as
often as by name ("corner wale Rizvi sahib", "teen number wali family"). They correct mistakes
quickly and expect the AI to understand household-shorthand item references without a formal
product catalog lookup every time.

# Customer Behaviour

Regular khata customers often ask "kitna ho gaya is mahine ka" (how much has this month come to)
before deciding what else to add to the tab. Children/informal buyers rarely interact with the
accounting side at all — the owner enters it after the fact from memory. Households settling
month-end frequently pay a round number that's close to but not exactly their balance ("5000 rakh
lo, baaki agle mahine") and expect that to be tracked as a specific partial payment, not assumed
to zero the account.

# Common Payment Behaviour

Overwhelming cash for day-to-day purchases. Khata (monthly running credit) is the main
non-cash pattern, settled once around salary time, often as one lump sum against the whole
month's accumulated small purchases rather than against individual line items. Mobile wallet
(easypaisa/jazzcash) payments are increasingly common for slightly larger purchases or bill
payments processed through the shop.

# Outstanding Balance Behaviour

Owners track khata customers closely around month-end specifically, since this is when the money
actually needs to be collected to restock. A very common request pattern is "sab ka is mahine ka
hisaab nikaalo" (work out everyone's balance for this month) right before month-end, effectively
an aggregate/period query across all credit customers at once.

# Clarification Rules

- If a customer's "ek packet [item]" or "usual" order has no single clear matching product in
  their own recent history, ask which specific item/brand rather than guessing a default.
- If a lump-sum khata payment is stated at month-end and doesn't match the customer's exact
  current balance, treat it as a partial (or, rarely, over-) payment of that stated amount — never
  silently assume it zeroes the balance unless the owner explicitly says "poora"/"clear"/"khatam".
- Never itemize a cash walk-in sale into invented individual products if the owner only gave a
  total — record it as a lump sale unless items were actually named.
- If "chutta" (change) is mentioned in a way that could affect the recorded amount (e.g., "usko
  chutta nahi tha to 50 zyada de diya, wapis lena hai") ask for clarification on the net figure
  rather than guess the adjustment.

# Frequently Forgotten Information

Owners very often omit which regular customer a "usual order" instruction refers to when
dictating quickly between other customers, whether a month-end payment is meant to fully clear
the tab or is a partial round-number payment, and the specific brand/size when naming a generic
category item ("do biscuit" without saying which brand, when the shop stocks several the customer
regularly alternates between).

# Voice Examples

"Rizvi sahib ka is mahine... paanch hazar... nahi chhe hazar hua hai" (a self-corrected running
total, common when an owner is calculating aloud rather than reading from the app). "Bacha aya
tha... do biscuit liye... [pause] ...Sooper wale" (a child's purchase recorded after the fact,
brand named after a pause). "Chutta nahi tha... pachas zyada de diya... wapis lena" (change
wasn't available, gave 50 extra, needs to be taken back — a running-balance nuance).

# Conversation Examples

**1. Single small cash sale, no customer link needed**
Owner: "Ek chips becha, 50 rupay."
AI records a simple cash sale total with no customer record required — most General Store cash
sales are genuinely anonymous.

**2. Khata entry for a known household**
Owner: "Rizvi sahib ke khata mein likh do, 200 ka saman liya."
AI drafts a credit sale of Rs. 200 against Rizvi sahib's account, asking for item detail only if
the owner wants to itemize (many won't for routine small purchases).

**3. Month-end lump-sum khata payment, exact match**
Owner: "Rizvi sahib ne is mahine ka poora hisaab kar diya."
AI resolves "poora hisaab" (full account) to Rizvi sahib's real current balance server-side and
drafts a full-balance payment.

**4. Month-end lump-sum, stated amount doesn't match balance exactly**
Owner: "5000 de diye Rizvi sahib ne, baaki agle mahine."
AI records exactly Rs. 5,000 as a partial payment — does not assume the balance is now zero even
though the owner called it a month-end settlement.

**5. Balance inquiry across all credit customers at once (month-end aggregate)**
Owner: "Sab ka is mahine ka hisaab nikaalo."
AI treats this as a whole-business aggregate query — the real outstanding balance for every
credit customer, not just the most recently mentioned one.

**6. Single balance inquiry**
Owner: "Rizvi sahib ka kitna baaki hai?"
AI answers with Rizvi sahib's real current ledger balance.

**7. Cigarette sold by single stick**
Owner: "Ek sutta becha, 20 rupay."
AI records a cash sale of a single cigarette item — recognizing "sutta" as the trade term for one
stick, not a whole pack.

**8. Ambiguous "usual order" needing clarification**
Owner: "Aslam ko uska rozana wala de do khate mein."
Aslam's recent history has both bread and eggs bought almost daily with no single dominant
pattern — AI asks which item(s) "rozana wala" (the daily one) refers to.

**9. Egg purchase in dozens**
Owner: "Ek dozen anda de do, khata mein."
AI records 12 eggs as one line item on the credit account, resolving "ek dozen" correctly to 12,
not 1.

**10. Half-dozen variant**
Owner: "Half dozen anda chahiye."
AI resolves to 6 eggs, distinguishing from the full-dozen phrasing above.

**11. Correction to a same-day khata entry**
Owner: "Rizvi sahib wali entry mein 200 nahi, 250 tha."
AI finds today's matching entry for Rizvi sahib and proposes a corrected amount, asking which
entry if more than one plausible match exists today.

**12. Item quantity correction**
Owner: "2 packet nahi, 3 packet the biscuit ke."
AI edits the quantity on the matched recent entry, recalculating the total accordingly.

**13. Advance/pre-payment before a known regular's usual monthly purchases**
Owner: "Rizvi sahib ne 2000 advance de diye is mahine ke liye."
AI records this as a payment/credit against the account now, ahead of the month's purchases being
itemized, rather than requiring a matching sale to exist first.

**14. Discount on a bulk small-institution order**
Owner: "Office wale order pe 100 discount de diya."
AI reduces the recorded total by the stated discount as an explicit adjustment, not by silently
altering individual item prices.

**15. Multiple different items in a single dictated sale**
Owner: "Rizvi sahib ko 1 kg chai, 2 kg cheeni, aur ek packet biscuit likh do, total 850."
AI drafts one sale with three line items whose combined total must reconcile to Rs. 850 exactly —
asks if it doesn't.

**16. Return of a wrong/spoiled item**
Owner: "Wo doodh wapas aya, kharab tha."
AI records a return of that item at its original sale price — a spoilage/defect return, not a
renegotiated price.

**17. Voice transcript with a mid-sentence self-correction**
Transcribed voice: "Rizvi sahib ka is mahine paanch — nahi chhe hazar hua hai."
AI uses the corrected figure (six thousand), treating "nahi" as an explicit correction marker for
what was just said.

**18. Roman Urdu date variant in a query**
Owner: "10 tarikh se ab tak Rizvi sahib ne kitna liya?"
AI resolves "tarikh" the same as "tareek"/"tareekh" and answers from the real entries in that
date range, not from memory.

**19. Mixed Urdu/English request**
Owner: "Please Rizvi sahib ka this month ka statement send kar do."
Treated identically to an all-Roman-Urdu equivalent — prepares/sends that customer's statement
for the current period.

**20. Follow-up referring to "that" entry from earlier in the conversation**
Owner: "Us wali entry ka amount change kar do, 250 nahi 300 tha."
AI uses the current active draft/most recently discussed entry to resolve "us wali" (that one)
without asking the owner to re-identify it from scratch.

**21. Ambiguous change/chutta adjustment**
Owner: "Usko chutta nahi tha, 50 zyada de diya, wapis lena hai agli baar."
Genuinely ambiguous how this should net against the ledger — AI asks whether to record this as a
50-rupee credit adjustment now or simply a note for next time, rather than silently picking one.

**22. New credit customer being set up mid-conversation**
Owner: "Naya banda hai, Farhan naam, usko khata khol do, 300 ka saman diya."
AI recognizes a new customer record is needed before the credit sale can be linked and proposes
both — customer creation, then the sale — rather than recording untraceable credit.

**23. Trade-specific edge case: mobile load / bill-payment sold as a service, not a product**
Owner: "Rizvi sahib ko 500 ka easyload diya, khata mein."
AI records this as a service sale of Rs. 500 against the account — same mechanics as a product
sale, just noting the item as the load/service provided.

**24. Owner asks for a daily cash total**
Owner: "Aaj ka naqad kitna hua?"
AI answers with today's real aggregate cash sales from the ledger, not an estimate.

**25. Owner corrects a mismatched customer**
Owner: "Nahi ye Rizvi sahib nahi, ye doosre corner wale Bhatti sahib hain."
AI treats this as an immediate correction to the customer link on the entry just discussed,
re-resolving to the real "Bhatti sahib" record rather than guessing or creating a duplicate.

**26. Multiple regular customers settling on the same day, asked about together**
Owner: "Aaj kis kis ne apna hisaab kiya?"
AI treats this as a period query for today across all customers, listing who made a payment today
and how much, grouped by customer.

**27. Owner references a customer only by location, not name**
Owner: "Corner wali dukaan ke pichhe wale ghar ka hisaab batao."
If this uniquely and unambiguously matches a customer note/reference already on record, AI
resolves it; if genuinely unclear, it asks who specifically is meant rather than guessing among
several possible households.

# Common AI Mistakes

Assuming a lump-sum month-end payment fully clears a khata balance when the stated amount doesn't
actually match the real balance. Itemizing a cash sale into invented specific products when the
owner only gave a total. Picking a "usual" item for a regular customer when their history
genuinely supports more than one candidate. Treating "chutta" (change) commentary as if it always
changes the recorded sale amount, when sometimes it's just a side remark.

# Best Practices

Keep genuinely anonymous cash sales simple (a total, no forced customer link) rather than
inventing detail the owner didn't give. Always resolve a stated month-end payment against the
real current balance rather than assuming intent from wording alone. For "usual order" requests,
check the specific named customer's own recent history first, and ask rather than guess when it's
genuinely ambiguous. Treat aggregate "sab ka hisaab" requests as whole-business period queries,
not a single customer's.
