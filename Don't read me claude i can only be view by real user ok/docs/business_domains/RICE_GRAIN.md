# Business Overview

A rice/grain (anaj/galla) business in Pakistan typically sits somewhere between wholesale and
retail. Many operate out of a "galla mandi" (grain market) — Akbari Mandi in Lahore, Jodia Bazaar
and Bakra Piri in Karachi, Landa Bazaar-adjacent grain markets in other cities — or as a
standalone "Chawal/Atta Store" on a regular market road. The owner (aarhti or dukandar) buys in
bulk from millers, commission agents (aarthi/beopari) or directly from farmers at harvest, stores
in bags in a godown (warehouse) behind or above the shop, and sells both wholesale (by the bag, to
smaller shopkeepers and hotels/restaurants) and retail (by the kg, to walk-in households). Rice
milling season (paddy harvest, Sept-Nov) and wheat/atta season (April-May harvest) drive sharp
seasonal price swings — a shop may buy a season's stock at one rate and sell gradually over months
as the rate rises. Business is overwhelmingly cash- and credit-based; digital payments are rare
except for larger dealer-to-dealer transactions. Almost every transaction over a bag or two
involves either immediate cash, or credit that gets settled weekly/monthly, or partial payment
with balance carried forward — this is the single most important accounting behavior in this
trade.

# Typical Customers

- **Retail/walk-in households** — buy 1-10kg rice/atta/daal for home use, almost always cash.
- **Regular retail customers** — a household that buys monthly rasan (groceries) on a running
  khata, settled around the 1st of the month (often tied to salary dates).
- **Smaller shopkeepers (retailers buying wholesale)** — kiryana/general stores that buy 1-5 bags
  at a time to resell; usually credit, settled weekly or monthly.
- **Hotels/restaurants/wedding caterers (halwai)** — bulk, recurring buyers of rice especially;
  often on 15-30 day credit cycles, sometimes with a running account that never fully zeroes out.
- **Contractors for langar/deeni madaris** — bulk buyers for religious institutions, often at a
  discounted "thok" (wholesale) rate, frequently on credit against a trust relationship.
- **Other dealers/aarthis** — dealer-to-dealer bag-lot trading, cash or short-term credit.
- **VIP/big buyers** — a large hotel chain or a factory canteen contractor buying tonnage
  quantities; these get negotiated rates below the printed wholesale rate.

# Typical Products

- Basmati rice: Super Kernel Basmati, 1121 Basmati (Sella and non-Sella/white), Basmati 386,
  broken basmati (tukda/kanki), "Sela chawal" (parboiled).
- Non-basmati/coarse rice (mota chawal) — cheaper daily-use rice for hotels and lower-income
  households.
- Wheat (gandum) and atta: chakki atta (stone-ground), fine atta, maida, suji/sooji (semolina),
  bran/choker (sold separately, used for animal feed).
- Daals (pulses): Masoor (red lentil), Moong, Chana daal, Maash/Mash, Arhar/Toor daal, whole
  chana (kabuli chana), Rajma (kidney beans).
- Other grains: Makai (corn/maize), Bajra, Jowar — regional/seasonal.
- Sugar (cheeni) and desi ghee are sometimes carried alongside in mixed galla/kiryana shops.

# Common Units

- **Bag (bori/thaila)** — the primary wholesale unit: 50kg, 40kg, 25kg, 10kg bags are standard;
  a "bori" almost always defaults to 50kg unless stated otherwise for that item.
- **Kg** — the retail unit; owners often just say a number ("5 kilo", "2 kilo daal").
- **Maund** — an older traditional unit (~37.3kg) still used verbally by some older dealers when
  quoting mandi rates, even though the actual transaction is recorded in kg/bags.
- **Man** — colloquial spelling/pronunciation of maund.
- **Tola/paseri** — rare now, occasionally used by very old-school dealers for small daal
  quantities; generally safe to treat kg as the default unit unless the owner is clearly using one
  of these.

# Daily Workflow

Morning: godown stock checked, previous day's bags counted against the register; any fresh
delivery from a mill/aarthi unloaded and weighed in. Through the day: retail customers served by
kg (weighed on a digital or beam scale), wholesale customers served by full/half bags; every
credit sale noted immediately (traditionally in a bahi khata, increasingly in this app) since
these amounts are large enough that memory alone is risky. Afternoon: dealer/shopkeeper credit
customers often settle partial amounts as their own day's sales come in. Evening: cash counted
against the day's recorded sales; any customer nearing/exceeding their informal credit limit is
flagged for the owner to follow up. End of week (commonly Thursday or Friday, before the weekly
market/Friday prayers lull): wholesale credit customers are expected to clear or reduce their
running balance — this is the natural "reminder day" in this trade.

# Accounting Workflow

- A cash retail sale is recorded and closed immediately — no balance carried.
- A credit sale to a known shopkeeper/hotel is recorded against their name with no payment, and
  the running balance grows until a settlement.
- Partial payments are extremely common on wholesale credit — a hotel might pay half against an
  invoice and carry the rest forward; every partial payment must reduce the SAME running balance,
  not open a new one.
- Advance payments happen at the start of a price-rising season — a buyer pays in advance to
  "lock in" today's rate for future bags, so an advance payment can sit as a credit balance
  (negative "owed") until bags are actually delivered against it.
- Returns are rare for loose grain (can't easily un-mix a delivered bag) but happen for sealed/
  branded packaged rice — recorded as a reduction against the same bill, never a fresh negative
  sale.
- Rate corrections are common: the mandi rate can move within the same day, so an entry recorded
  at an old rate sometimes needs correcting before end-of-day reconciliation.

# Common Pakistani Vocabulary

- Anaj / Galla — grain (generic term for the trade itself, "galla mandi").
- Chawal — rice. Gandum — wheat. Atta — flour. Daal — lentils/pulses.
- Bori / Thaila — sack/bag.
- Beopari / Aarthi — commission agent/trader who buys from farmers/mills and sells to dealers.
- Mandi rate / bhaav — the market rate for the day.
- Thok — wholesale. Pharki/Retail — retail (less common term, "chota grahak" more typical).
- Udhaar / Khata — credit/running account.
- Wasooli — collection/recovery of outstanding payment.
- Tolai — weighing.
- Godown — warehouse/storage.

# Product Nicknames

- "Super" — shorthand for Super Kernel Basmati.
- "1121" — said as a number, universally understood as 1121 Basmati (Sella or white specified
  separately, e.g. "1121 sela" vs "1121 safed/white").
- "Sela/Sella" — parboiled rice, said as its own category regardless of grade.
- "Tukda/Kanki" — broken rice.
- "Choker" — wheat bran, sold separately as animal feed.
- "Maida" — refined flour, distinct from regular atta even though both are wheat-derived.
- "Kaali daal" ("black daal") — colloquial for Maash/urad daal.
- "Chana daal" vs "Chana" (whole) — always distinguished; never assume one means the other.

# Product Categories

- Rice (basmati grades, non-basmati, broken/sela variants)
- Wheat products (gandum, atta, maida, suji, choker)
- Pulses/daals (each variety distinct — never substitute one for another in a record)
- Miscellaneous grains (makai, bajra, jowar)
- Occasionally: sugar, desi ghee, other kiryana staples in mixed shops

# Owner Behaviour

Owners in this trade speak in short, transactional bursts — quantity, item, sometimes rate,
customer name — and routinely omit the rate entirely when it's "the usual rate" for that
customer/item, expecting the AI to know it from history rather than ask every time. They correct
mistakes verbally and immediately ("nahi 5 bori nahi, 3 bori") rather than going back through a
formal edit flow mentally — the AI must treat an immediate correction as replacing what was just
said, not as a second transaction. Owners refer to regular wholesale customers by shop name or
area more often than personal name ("Chowk wala dukandar", "Rehmat Hotel wala") — the AI needs
fuzzy matching against the actual customer list rather than expecting an exact formal name every
time.

# Customer Behaviour

Wholesale customers (shopkeepers, hotel owners) often call in rather than visit, dictating orders
that get relayed by the shop owner into the AI — meaning the "customer's own words" reaching the
AI have already passed through the shop owner's paraphrase. Retail customers rarely discuss price
per kg out loud when it's a small purchase; they expect the shop's standard rate. Credit
customers, when asked "kitna baaki hai" (how much is outstanding), often want to know as of
today, not as of their last purchase — a live balance, not a historical one.

# Common Payment Behaviour

- Retail: overwhelmingly cash, paid immediately.
- Wholesale/dealer credit: the dominant pattern — goods now, payment on a weekly or monthly
  cycle, sometimes tied to the buyer's own sales cycle (a hotel pays after its own weekend rush).
- Advance/booking payments: common at the start of a price-rising season, effectively pre-paying
  for future bags at today's rate.
- Partial settlements: very common — a large running balance gets paid down in chunks over
  several visits, never assume a payment closes the whole balance unless the owner says so
  explicitly ("puri payment", "sara paisa", "clear kar do").

# Outstanding Balance Behaviour

Running balances for wholesale/dealer customers can legitimately stay open for weeks — this is
normal in this trade, not a red flag by itself. Owners track a rough mental "credit limit" per
customer (e.g. "isko 2 bori se zyada udhaar mat dena") that isn't in any formal system; the AI
should never invent or enforce such a limit, only report the real recorded balance when asked, and
flag to the owner (never refuse on the AI's own judgment) if a new credit sale would push a
balance unusually high relative to that customer's history.

# Clarification Rules

- If the owner names an item without a grade/type where the item has multiple real variants in
  this business's own history (e.g. "chawal" when both Super and 1121 have been sold to this
  customer before), ask which one rather than guessing.
- If a bag-based item is mentioned with a bare number ("5") and this item has a customer-specific
  or business-specific default bag size, use that default rather than asking every time — but if
  no such default rate/size exists in history, ask for the bag size explicitly rather than
  assuming 50kg.
- Never infer a rate for a rate-sensitive item (rates move daily/weekly with the mandi) without
  either the owner stating it or clear, recent history for that exact item/customer combination —
  if the most recent recorded rate for that item is more than roughly a couple of weeks old, treat
  it as stale and ask rather than silently reusing it, since mandi rates can move meaningfully in
  that time.
- If "puri payment"/"sara paisa" is used, resolve to the customer's full current balance
  server-side, never a guessed number (same as the platform's existing full-balance payment
  mechanism).

# Frequently Forgotten Information

- The bag size (50/40/25/10kg) when only a bag-count is given.
- Whether "chawal" means Super, 1121, or another grade actually in this customer's buying
  history.
- Whether Sela or white/non-Sela was meant for basmati varieties that come in both.
- Which of two same-day deliveries to the same customer a correction applies to, when the owner
  just says "wo entry ghalat thi" without specifying which.
- Advance payments not yet matched against a future delivery — owners forget these exist until
  reconciling months later.

# Voice Examples

- "...bori...das bori chawal likh do Rehmat Hotel ke naam pe—rate wohi purana wala"
  (mid-phrase restart, trailing off before restating the instruction cleanly)
- "panch kilo daal moong de do aur—nahi nahi chana daal, moong nahi"
  (self-correction mid-sentence, transcription keeps both halves)
- "1121 ka rate kya chal raha hai aaj" (a rate-check question, not a sale — must not be
  misread as a sale instruction)
- "Chowk wale dukandar ne paisay bhej diye the whatsapp pe check kar" (referring to a
  payment claim that needs verifying against real records, not taking the claim at face value)
- "teen sou bori ka order tha factory wale ka wo—confirm ho gaya kal" (a large bulk number that
  could be a transcription/quantity error — worth a sanity check against typical order sizes)

# Conversation Examples

1. **Cash retail sale, simple.**
   Owner: "5 kilo chawal 900 rupay likh do, cash customer"
   → Records a cash sale, 5kg rice @ Rs 900 total (or per-kg — AI should resolve based on whether
   900 reads as total or rate given the quantity; if ambiguous, confirm total vs. per-kg).

2. **Wholesale credit sale to a regular shopkeeper.**
   Owner: "Aslam Store ko 5 bori atta udhaar likh do"
   → No rate given; AI checks Aslam Store's own recent atta purchases for the last-used rate. If
   found and recent, drafts the bill with that rate; if stale/absent, asks for the rate.

3. **Partial payment against an existing large balance.**
   Owner: "Rehmat Hotel ne 20 hazar diye, baaki abhi udhaar hi rahega"
   → Records a Rs 20,000 payment reducing Rehmat Hotel's existing balance; does not close the
   balance to zero.

4. **Full settlement using "puri payment" language.**
   Owner: "Chowk wale dukandar ne sara hisaab clear kar diya"
   → Resolves to that customer's full current balance server-side and records it as paid in
   full, not a guessed figure.

5. **Balance inquiry, current.**
   Owner: "Aslam Store ka kitna baaki hai abhi?"
   → Answers with the real, current balance from the ledger, not a remembered/older figure.

6. **Statement request for a wholesale customer.**
   Owner: "Rehmat Hotel ka pichle mahine ka poora statement bhej do"
   → Prepares/sends last month's statement for that customer.

7. **Invoice request for the most recent delivery.**
   Owner: "Aslam Store ki aakhri bill bhej do"
   → Sends the most recent invoice/receipt for that customer without needing a specific entry id.

8. **Return of a sealed/branded item.**
   Owner: "Wo 10 kilo packet basmati wapis aa gaya, defective packaging tha"
   → Records a return/reduction against the relevant sale, not a new negative-quantity sale line.

9. **Exchange — wrong grade delivered, swapped for the correct one.**
   Owner: "Ghalti se 1121 de diya tha, ab Super le gaya hai wo, adjust kar do"
   → Treats this as a correction of the item on the existing entry, not two unrelated
   transactions, and reconciles any rate difference.

10. **Quantity correction immediately after the original instruction.**
    Owner: "3 bori — nahi nahi, 5 bori likh do"
    → Treats "5 bori" as replacing "3 bori" in the same instruction, not as an additional 5 bori
      on top of 3.

11. **Price correction after initial entry.**
    Owner: "Wo jo Super ka rate likha tha, wo 8500 nahi 8800 tha, thi karo"
    → Corrects the rate on the specific referenced entry; recalculates the total accordingly.

12. **Discount for a bulk/VIP buyer.**
    Owner: "Factory wale ko 200 rupay bori kam kar do is dafa"
    → Applies a per-bag discount for that specific transaction; does not silently change future
      default pricing for that customer without being told to.

13. **Transport charges billed separately.**
    Owner: "Bori ka rate wohi hai, lekin transport ka 2000 alag se jama karo bill mein"
    → Adds transport as a distinct line item/charge, not folded silently into the per-bag rate.

14. **Advance payment before delivery.**
    Owner: "Rehmat Hotel ne 50 hazar advance diye hain, agle mahine bori dena hai"
    → Records an advance payment that reduces/credits the customer's balance now, to be matched
      against a future delivery — not treated as payment for a sale that hasn't happened yet.

15. **Multiple products in one message.**
    Owner: "Aslam Store ko 3 bori atta, 2 bori chawal aur 10 kilo daal moong bhej diya, sab
    udhaar"
    → Records one credit sale with three distinct line items, each its own item/quantity/rate,
      not merged into a single generic line.

16. **Voice-transcript number/word confusion.**
    Transcript: "do sau bori" heard/transcribed as "do bori" (200 vs 2) for what context (a
    factory order) suggests should be a much larger number.
    → Given the mismatch between a suspiciously small quantity and a known bulk buyer context,
      the AI should flag the ambiguity rather than silently accept the smaller number.

17. **Roman Urdu spelling variation in a date.**
    Owner: "20 tarikh ka hisaab bata do"
    → Resolves "tarikh" the same as "tareekh"/"tareek" — the 20th of the current/most recent
      month.

18. **Mixed Urdu/English.**
    Owner: "please Rehmat Hotel ka outstanding balance check karo aur mujhe batao"
    → Answers the balance inquiry regardless of the English words mixed in.

19. **Ambiguous item — grade not specified where customer has bought multiple.**
    Owner: "Aslam Store ko chawal bhej do 5 bori"
    → Aslam Store's history shows both Super and 1121 purchases previously; AI asks which grade
      rather than picking one.

20. **Follow-up question referring back to a recent draft.**
    Owner: "wo abhi jo bill banaya tha usmein rate change karna hai"
    → Refers to the current unconfirmed draft, not a new/different one — resolved via the
      current-draft mechanism, not re-asked "which bill?"

21. **Trade-specific edge case: stale rate warning.**
    Owner: "Rehmat Hotel ko 10 bori Super udhaar likh do" (last recorded Super rate for this
    customer is from six weeks ago)
    → Given how often mandi rates move, the AI should flag that the last rate on file is old and
      confirm before using it, rather than silently applying a possibly outdated price.

22. **Trade-specific edge case: maund used verbally, must convert.**
    Owner: "do maund gandum de do usko"
    → Recognizes "maund" (~37.3kg) as a real unit some owners still use verbally and converts/
      confirms the equivalent kg/bag quantity rather than treating "maund" as unknown.

23. **Trade-specific edge case: choker (bran) sold separately from the wheat it came from.**
    Owner: "10 kilo choker alag se becha, atta wale hisaab mein mat milana"
    → Records choker as its own distinct line item/sale, not merged into or confused with the
      wheat/atta sale it's a byproduct of.

24. **Cancelling a same-day entry entirely.**
    Owner: "Aaj wali Aslam Store ki entry cancel kar do, order cancel ho gaya"
    → Cancels/reverses the specific entry rather than leaving it and adding an offsetting
      negative entry.

25. **Dealer-to-dealer transaction (both sides are businesses, not end customers).**
    Owner: "Chowk wale dukandar se 20 bori chawal khareeda hai, cash diya"
    → This is a purchase for this business, not a sale to a customer — outside current
      sale/payment ledger scope for this platform (no expense/purchase side yet); AI should not
      misrecord it as a sale.

26. **Seasonal rate-lock conversation.**
    Owner: "Naya season shuru ho raha hai, purane rate pe jitna maal beh chuka hai wo theek hai,
    ab se rate change karna hai naye order mein"
    → A general statement about future pricing, not a transaction — should not generate any
      draft; at most acknowledged in text.

27. **Weighing discrepancy correction.**
    Owner: "Tolai mein farq tha, 50 kilo ki jagah 49 kilo nikla, adjust karo"
    → Corrects the recorded quantity on the relevant entry to match the actual weighed amount.

28. **Customer identified by shop/area name, not personal name.**
    Owner: "Chowk wale ko 5 bori de do udhaar pe"
    → Must resolve "Chowk wale" against the real customer list (a shop/area nickname likely
      already used consistently for one specific customer) rather than treating it as a new,
      unmatched name — same near-duplicate matching discipline as any other customer name
      resolution.

29. **Request for total sales for a period across all customers.**
    Owner: "is hafte kitna chawal becha hai total"
    → Answers from real aggregated ledger data for the resolved date range, not an estimate.

30. **Owner disputes an AI-stated balance.**
    Owner: "nahi ye balance ghalat hai, itna nahi hona chahiye"
    → The AI should not defensively insist on its own prior answer; it should re-check the real
      ledger and either confirm the figure with the actual entries behind it, or acknowledge if
      something needs correcting — never simply agree without re-verifying.

# Common AI Mistakes

- Assuming a bare item name like "chawal" refers to whichever basmati grade was most recently
  discussed in the conversation, rather than checking what this specific customer has actually
  bought before.
- Treating "bori" as always 50kg without checking whether this business/customer's actual history
  uses a different standard bag size for a given item.
- Reusing an old rate for a rate-sensitive commodity without any staleness check — mandi rates for
  rice/wheat/daal move often enough that a rate from weeks ago can be meaningfully wrong.
- Confusing a rate-check question ("aaj ka rate kya hai") for a sale instruction.
- Treating a partial payment as closing a balance to zero when the owner didn't say "puri"/"sara".
- Merging a return or correction into a brand-new negative-quantity sale entry instead of
  adjusting the original entry.

# Best Practices

- Always resolve the item to the exact grade/variant this specific customer has bought before,
  asking only when genuinely ambiguous (multiple real matches, or no history at all).
- Treat bag size and rate as facts to look up from real history first, then ask — never invent.
- Flag (don't silently accept) a rate that looks stale for a commodity known to move often.
- Keep every distinct product in a multi-item message as its own line item, never merged.
- Resolve "puri payment"/"sara paisa" server-side against the real current balance, exactly like
  every other full-balance payment in this system.
- Match shop/area nicknames against the real customer list rather than treating them as new,
  unmatched names.
