# Business Overview

A Pakistani garments **metal accessories / trims** supplier — sells the small metal (and metal-adjacent) hardware that goes onto stitched garments during manufacturing: eyelets, metal cord-ends/capsules, buckles, drawstring tipping (dori/hoodi tipping), snap/press buttons, rivets, D-rings, sliders, zip pullers, prong studs, and similar. This is fundamentally a **B2B wholesale/manufacturing-supply business**, not a retail clothing shop — customers are garment factories, stitching units, hoodie/trouser/jacket manufacturers, and export-order subcontractors, not end consumers buying clothes. Common in markets like Karachi's Bolton Market/Boulton Market, Lahore's garment-trim wholesale clusters, and Sialkot/Faisalabad's export-adjacent supply chains. Business is typically family-run, often multi-generational (father-to-son handover is common in this exact trade), with one counter handling walk-in factory buyers and phone/WhatsApp orders from regular factory accounts simultaneously. Items are small, cheap per-unit, and sold in very large bulk counts (thousands of pieces per order) — a single order commonly runs into tens or hundreds of thousands of rupees even though each piece costs a few rupees. Export-order-linked factories buy in large, time-pressured batches (a shipment deadline drives urgency); local-market factories buy in smaller, more frequent repeat orders. Credit is the norm with established factory customers — cash on the counter is more the exception (small/new/one-off buyers) than the rule.

# Typical Customers

- **Garment/hoodie/jacket factories** — the core customer base; place recurring bulk orders (gross/thousand-piece quantities), almost always on credit with periodic settlement.
- **Stitching units/subcontractors** — smaller operations doing job work for bigger factories or exporters; buy smaller quantities more frequently, mixed cash/credit.
- **Export-order buyers** — factories fulfilling a foreign buyer's order; time-pressured, often need an exact matching finish/color to a sample, larger one-time bulk orders.
- **Local retail garment units** — smaller shops that also do minor stitching/alteration and need small quantities of buttons/snaps.
- **Walk-in factory purchasing staff** — a factory's own buyer/karigar sent to physically pick items and compare finishes/samples before ordering.
- **Regular/repeat accounts** — the majority of real revenue; known by name, known credit terms, known typical order pattern.
- **New/one-off buyers** — usually cash, no account yet, often testing quality/price before becoming a regular account.

# Typical Products

Eyelets (all sizes, brass/nickel/antique finishes), metal cord-ends/cord-locks ("capsule" — used at the end of a drawstring/dori), buckles (belt buckles, bag buckles, adjustable strap buckles), dori/hoodi tipping (the metal or plastic-metal tip crimped onto the end of a drawstring so it doesn't fray — "aglet"), snap buttons/press buttons (jeans buttons, kids'-wear snaps, spring snaps), rivets (jeans rivets, denim rivets), D-rings, O-rings, sliders (for adjustable straps), zip pullers/zip heads, prong studs/eyelet washers, hooks and eyes, cord stoppers, chicago screws, and general "metal accessories" catch-all items a factory asks for by sample rather than by exact name.

# Common Units

- **Gross (12 dozen = 144 pieces)** — the default wholesale unit for buttons, eyelets, snaps.
- **Dozen** — smaller repeat orders, sample orders.
- **Piece/pc (adad)** — for buckles, larger hardware items, or when quoting unit price.
- **Box/Packet** — items are physically packed and sold by box count (e.g. "1 box = 12 gross"), so "kitne box" and "kitne gross" are both live units in the same conversation.
- **Thousand (hazar)** — very large factory orders are often spoken in thousands directly ("10 hazar eyelet") rather than converted to gross.
- **Set** — a buckle/D-ring/slider that's sold as a matched set for one strap.
- **Card/Sheet** — some small hardware (snap buttons) comes on a card of a fixed count.

# Daily Workflow

Morning: shop opens, counter staff check overnight WhatsApp orders from factory buyers (a very common ordering channel — a factory's purchase person sends a voice note or photo of the sample they need matched). Walk-in factory buyers arrive through the day comparing finishes/samples against what they need, often haggling per-gross rate for large quantities. Phone/WhatsApp orders are confirmed against stock, packed, and either handed to the factory's own pickup rider or sent via a local courier/rider service (transport charge separately noted). Credit sales to known factory accounts are the majority of volume; cash sales are smaller walk-in/one-off buyers. Afternoon: supplier-side receiving (new stock of eyelets/buttons/buckles arriving from the importer/manufacturer side of the business, if this shop also imports/manufactures). Evening: closing the day's cash, reconciling what went out on credit against which factory account, following up on any account that's due for its periodic settlement (weekly/bi-weekly/monthly, per that factory's agreed terms) — end of day.

# Accounting Workflow

- **Sales**: almost always multi-item (a factory rarely orders just one thing) — eyelets + tipping + snap buttons in one order is completely normal and should be drafted as one bill with multiple line items, not one bill per item.
- **Credit**: the default assumption for an established factory account unless cash is explicitly mentioned — owners routinely say only the item/quantity/rate and never say the word "credit" or "udhaar" at all, because for a regular factory account it's simply understood.
- **Cash**: stated explicitly ("cash le liya", "nagad") — usually a smaller/new/one-off buyer.
- **Advance**: an export-order factory sometimes pays a token advance to lock in a bulk order before it's fully produced/packed — recorded distinctly from a completed sale.
- **Partial payments**: very common on large factory orders — a factory pays part now, balance on a later settlement date tied to their own production/export cycle.
- **Outstanding balances**: tracked per factory account, often informally called "khata" even though it's a proper B2B trade credit relationship, not a small personal loan.
- **Returns**: a factory rejecting a batch for finish/color mismatch against their sample — distinct from a simple quantity correction, and needs its own entry against the original sale, not silently overwritten.
- **Corrections**: quantity/rate typos are common given the large numbers involved (a "10" vs "100" gross typo is a real, expensive risk) — the AI must be especially careful confirming large quantities back to the owner before drafting.

# Product Nicknames

"Capsule" for a metal cord-end/cord-lock (not a medical capsule — this is a fixed, common trade nickname). "Tipping" for the aglet crimped onto a drawstring end. "Button" used generically for any snap/press button even when the specific type (jeans button vs. kids' snap) differs. "Set" for a matched buckle+D-ring+slider combo used on one strap. Some shops nickname a specific finish informally ("antique wala", "chocolate wala" for a dark bronze finish, "gold wala", "chandi wala" for silver/nickel finish) rather than using a supplier's technical finish code.

# Product Categories

Fasteners/closures (snap buttons, hooks and eyes, Chicago screws), cord hardware (tipping, capsules/cord-locks, cord stoppers), strap hardware (buckles, D-rings, O-rings, sliders), eyelets and grommets, zip hardware (pullers/heads — distinct from the zip itself, which this business usually does not sell), rivets and studs.

# Owner Behaviour

Owners in this trade speak in dense, information-packed shorthand because both sides (owner and the factory buyer) already know the relationship — they routinely omit the customer's full name (using a factory nickname or just "wo factory wala"), omit the unit when it's "obviously" gross for that item, and omit "credit" entirely for an established account since it's the default. Corrections are common and blunt — "nahi 10 gross nahi, 100 gross tha" — and must be treated as an urgent correction to the JUST-drafted entry, not a new order. Multiple items are routinely given in one breath/message ("500 gross eyelet, 200 gross tipping, 50 gross button — X factory ko"), and the owner expects one bill covering all of them, not three separate ones.

# Customer Behaviour

Factory buyers often text/voice-note with a sample photo and expect the shop owner (relaying through the AI) to match it against known stock rather than describing the item themselves in words at all — this is a case where the AI genuinely cannot resolve the item from text alone and must let the owner say what he decided after physically matching the sample, not guess a generic "eyelet". Regular factory accounts ask for their running balance ("hamara kitna baaki hai") ahead of their own settlement cycle, expecting a fast, exact answer.

# Common Payment Behaviour

- **Cash** — smaller/new/walk-in buyers, stated explicitly.
- **Credit** — the default for established factory accounts; settlement is periodic (weekly/bi-weekly/monthly) rather than per-order.
- **Advance** — for large or export-linked bulk orders, a token advance secures the order before full production/packing.
- **Bank transfer** — increasingly common for larger factory settlements, generally described as "bank mein daal diya" or naming the bank directly.
- **Partial/staged payments** — large orders often get paid in 2-3 installments tied to delivery milestones (advance, on-dispatch, on-final-settlement).

# Outstanding Balance Behaviour

Outstanding balances in this trade can be large in absolute rupee terms purely because of volume (gross-quantity orders), even though each individual item is cheap — the AI must never treat a large number here as suspicious/likely-wrong without double-checking, since it's completely normal for this trade. Factory accounts are chased around their own settlement cycle, not randomly; "is hafte" (this week)/"is mahine" (this month) framing is common when an owner asks about a specific account's due balance.

# Clarification Rules

- **Ask** when the owner names only a partial/ambiguous descriptor for an item variant that has multiple real options in this business's own history (e.g. "button" when both jeans buttons and kids' snap buttons exist as separate priced items) — resolve via the customer's own history first, then the rest of the business, exactly as the general item-price-resolution mechanism already does; only ask if genuinely unresolved.
- **Ask** before drafting any quantity that looks like it may be a magnitude typo (a sudden "1000 gross" from a customer whose history shows orders in the tens of gross) — confirm rather than silently accept.
- **Infer** the unit as gross by default for small hardware items (eyelets, snaps, tipping) when the owner gives only a bare number and no unit, since that's this trade's overwhelming default — but only when the customer's own history supports it; state the assumption in the confirmation rather than silently assuming.
- **Never guess** a specific finish/color/size for an item when the owner's message doesn't specify one and history doesn't disambiguate — this is exactly the "matched against a physical sample" case that text alone cannot resolve; ask.
- **Never guess** whether an unstated payment method is cash or credit for a customer whose own history is mixed — ask, don't default silently either way.

# Frequently Forgotten Information

The specific finish/color when more than one exists for that item. The unit (gross vs. piece vs. box) when the owner is rushing. Which factory account a bulk order belongs to when two similarly-named factories exist. Whether a large order includes an advance already paid. The transport/rider charge for a dispatched order, which owners often forget to mention separately and later add as an afterthought.

# Conversation Examples

1. **Multi-item bulk credit sale to a known factory**
   Owner: "X factory ko 500 gross eyelet, 200 gross tipping likh do"
   → Draft one bill, two line items, customer matched to X factory, rate resolved from that factory's own recent history for each item, credit assumed (no cash/payment mentioned), confirm before saving.

2. **Cash sale, walk-in, single item**
   Owner: "cash sale — 5 gross buckle, 800 rupees"
   → No customer name given; ask which customer/whether this is a walk-in with no account, since even a cash sale needs a ledger entry tied to someone (or explicitly "walk-in/cash customer" if the business tracks those separately).

3. **Partial payment against a large factory order**
   Owner: "Y factory ne 50,000 mein se 20,000 de diye abhi"
   → Record a payment of 20,000 against Y factory's balance; do not touch the underlying sale entry.

4. **Full settlement / balance cleared**
   Owner: "Z factory ne apna poora hisaab saaf kar diya"
   → `draft_payment` with `full_balance: true`; resolve the real current balance server-side, never estimate it.

5. **Outstanding balance inquiry, specific account**
   Owner: "X factory ka kitna baaki hai"
   → Answer only from the real, current ledger balance for that matched customer — never recall/estimate from memory.

6. **Statement request for a factory account**
   Owner: "Y factory ko poora statement bhej do"
   → Prepare and send the full statement; never claim it has already been delivered, only that it's prepared/sending.

7. **Invoice request for the most recent order**
   Owner: "Z factory ka aakhri bill dobara bhej do"
   → Resend their most recent matching invoice; no need to know a specific invoice id.

8. **Return / rejected batch**
   Owner: "X factory ne wo pichla eyelet wala order wapas kar diya, finish match nahi hua"
   → This is a return against a specific prior sale, not a new negative-quantity sale — identify the matching entry and ask how the owner wants it handled (full reversal vs. partial) if not explicit.

9. **Exchange for a different finish**
   Owner: "wo antique wale buckle waapis, gold wale de diye usko"
   → An exchange — the original item is returned and a different variant sold; treat as two linked actions, not a silent overwrite of the original entry.

10. **Quantity correction immediately after a draft**
    Owner: "10 gross nahi, 100 gross tha"
    → Correct the just-drafted quantity on the SAME draft, not a new bill — this is exactly the "current active draft" case.

11. **Price correction after confirmation was already tapped**
    Owner: "wo rate galat tha, 45 nahi 40 tha"
    → Since the entry may already be confirmed, this becomes an edit request against the existing ledger entry, not a fresh draft — ask to confirm which entry if ambiguous.

12. **Discount for bulk order**
    Owner: "1000 gross se zyada pe 2 rupay kam kar diya rate"
    → Apply the stated adjusted rate directly to the draft; never silently apply a "usual" discount the owner didn't state this time.

13. **Transport/rider charge added separately**
    Owner: "maal bhej diya, 300 rider ka alag likh do"
    → A distinct small charge line, not folded silently into the item rate.

14. **Advance payment for an export-linked order**
    Owner: "export order ka advance le liya, 15000"
    → Record as an advance/payment against the (possibly not-yet-fully-drafted) order — flag it as an advance, not a completed sale.

15. **Multiple products, mixed units in one message**
    Owner: "200 gross snap button, 5 dozen buckle set, aur 50 piece D-ring bhi de do"
    → Three line items with three different units in one bill — each unit respected exactly as stated, not normalized incorrectly.

16. **Voice-transcript number/word confusion**
    Owner (voice): "do sau gross" transcribed ambiguously as "do so" or "dosa" by a speech engine
    → Treat "do sau" as 200 given the trade context (gross-quantity orders), but if genuinely ambiguous in a low-confidence transcript, confirm the number back before drafting.

17. **Roman Urdu spelling variation on a date**
    Owner: "5 tarkeeh ka bill dikhao"
    → Resolve "tarkeeh" as the same date word as "tareekh"/"tareek" — the 5th of the current/most recent applicable month.

18. **Mixed Urdu/English in one message**
    Owner: "please X factory ka invoice generate kar ke send kar do on whatsapp"
    → Understood identically to a fully Roman Urdu or fully English version of the same request.

19. **Ambiguous item requiring clarification (sample-matched item)**
    Owner: "wo jo sample dikhaya tha wahi wala bhej do 300 gross"
    → The AI cannot know which physical sample was shown in person — ask the owner to name the specific item/finish, since no text description exists to match against history.

20. **Follow-up question after a draft, referring to "it"**
    Owner: "aur uska rate kya tha pichli dafa"
    → "uska" refers to the item just discussed in this conversation — answer from real history for that specific item/customer, not a guess.

21. **New factory account, first order (creating a customer)**
    Owner: "naya factory hai, ABC Garments naam se account banao"
    → Propose a new customer; check for a near-duplicate name first (a similarly-named existing account) before creating.

22. **Two similarly-named factories — ambiguity**
    Owner: "Al-Noor ko bhej do" (business has both "Al-Noor Garments" and "Al-Noor Textile" as separate accounts)
    → Ask which one rather than guessing, since the names are genuinely close enough to be a real risk.

23. **Balance-of-payment on a staged order**
    Owner: "baaki wala paisa aa gaya us export order ka"
    → A follow-up/final payment on an order that already had an advance recorded — link it to the same customer's outstanding balance, not a fresh unrelated payment.

24. **Owner corrects a customer misattribution**
    Owner: "ye X factory ka nahi tha, Y factory ka tha, theek karo"
    → A transfer of the entry from X to Y, not a delete-and-recreate; use the transfer capability so both accounts' balances update correctly.

25. **Undo immediately after a mistaken confirm**
    Owner: "galat confirm ho gaya, wapas karo"
    → Undo the just-confirmed action within the valid window; if the window has passed, say so plainly rather than pretending it worked.

26. **Bare number with no unit stated, resolved from history**
    Owner: "X ko 500 eyelet de do"
    → Since eyelets are gross-denominated for this business by default and X's own history confirms it, resolve as 500 gross rather than 500 pieces — state that assumption in the confirmation.

27. **Item nickname requiring resolution**
    Owner: "capsule wale 200 gross bhej do Z ko"
    → "Capsule" resolves to the metal cord-end/cord-lock item in this business's own catalog, not a literal medical term.

28. **Query spanning a date range**
    Owner: "pichle hafte X factory ne kya kya liya"
    → A period-scoped, customer-scoped query — surfaced via the real ledger for that exact range, not recalled from memory.

29. **Whole-business period query, multiple factories**
    Owner: "is hafte kitna maal factories ko gaya, kis kis ko"
    → A whole-business period view across all customers for that range, not a single-customer answer.

30. **Owner asks whether something was sent on WhatsApp**
    Owner: "Y factory ko statement bhej diya tha kya"
    → Never assert delivery status was confirmed unless it genuinely was — say what's known (prepared/queued) and point to the real delivery status if uncertain.

31. **Large quantity that could be a typo, flagged before drafting**
    Owner: "10000 gross eyelet X ko" (X's history shows orders around 200-500 gross)
    → Flag the mismatch and confirm before drafting — a magnitude typo here is expensive and plausible.

32. **Rate not stated, resolved from a DIFFERENT customer's history**
    Owner: "naye factory Z ko 300 gross tipping de do" (Z has no prior orders for tipping)
    → Since Z's own history has no match, search the rest of the business for a recent tipping rate and use it if found; otherwise ask, never invent a number.

33. **Combined advance + partial delivery + balance due, described in one message**
    Owner: "export wale order ka 15000 advance tha, ab 30000 ka maal bheja hai, baaki 15000 baad mein"
    → A layered scenario: advance already recorded, a new sale for the delivered goods, and a note that the remaining balance is expected later — each piece recorded as its own real fact, not merged into one vague entry.

34. **Owner uses a trade term the AI must not literalize**
    Owner: "hoodi ka maal taiyar hai bhej do"
    → "Hoodi" here means hoodie-drawstring-related trims generically, not a literal single product named "hoodi" — resolve against the customer's typical order pattern/history for that account.

35. **Cancelling an entry entirely (not editing it)**
    Owner: "wo order cancel kar do, factory ne mana kar diya"
    → The order was never actually fulfilled — remove/cancel the entry rather than treating it as a return (which implies goods went out and came back); ask which specific entry if more than one recent draft could match.

# Common AI Mistakes

Treating an unstated unit as "pieces" by default instead of the trade's real default (gross) for small hardware. Silently inventing a finish/color when the owner's message and history both leave it genuinely ambiguous instead of asking. Splitting one multi-item order into several separate bills instead of one bill with multiple line items. Assuming cash when payment method wasn't stated for an established credit-account factory. Failing to flag an order quantity that's wildly out of line with that customer's normal pattern, and drafting it as-is. Confusing a return (goods physically came back) with a cancellation (order never went out) or an exchange (a different item replaced it) — these are three different real-world events and must not be collapsed into one generic "undo".

# Best Practices

Resolve item name, finish, and rate from THIS customer's own recent order history first, then the rest of the business, and only ask the owner when neither has a genuine match — never invent a number or a finish. Always draft multi-item orders as one bill with multiple line items when the owner names several items together. Treat large rupee totals as normal for this trade (bulk gross-quantity orders naturally produce large sums) rather than treating size alone as suspicious, while still flagging genuine magnitude mismatches against a specific customer's own typical order size. Keep advance payments, partial payments, and full settlements as distinct, correctly-typed ledger events. Never claim a WhatsApp send has completed unless delivery is actually confirmed. When a physical sample was involved in deciding an item/finish, accept that the AI cannot resolve it from text and ask the owner to state plainly what was decided, rather than guessing from a vague description.
