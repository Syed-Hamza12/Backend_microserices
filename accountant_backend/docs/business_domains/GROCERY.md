# Business Overview

A Pakistani grocery store (kiryana store) — a dedicated food/household-consumables retailer, distinct from a broader "general store" in that its core business is packaged and loose food items: rice, atta (flour), oil/ghee, spices/masala, tea, dairy, sugar, pulses (daal), soft drinks, and daily-use packaged goods. Ranges from a small neighborhood shop to a larger store supplying nearby smaller shops on a mini-wholesale basis. Heavily cash-and-credit driven — most neighborhood grocers keep an informal "khata" (running credit ledger) for regular households in the area, settled monthly (often tied to salary dates) rather than per-purchase. Stock is bought from wholesale markets (e.g. Akbari Mandi in Lahore, Jodia Bazaar in Karachi) or directly from distributors of major brands. Prices on loose/commodity items (rice, daal, sugar, oil) fluctuate often with market rates, so owners frequently need to update or confirm the current rate rather than relying on a fixed price.

# Typical Customers

- **Walk-in daily customers** — buy small daily-need quantities (half kg daal, 1 liter oil), usually cash.
- **Regular household customers (khata customers)** — same families buying repeatedly through the month, settled monthly, the backbone of most neighborhood grocers' credit book.
- **Small shopkeepers/mini-wholesale buyers** — buy larger quantities (a full bag of rice, a carton of ghee tins) to resell in their own smaller shop.
- **Restaurants/hotels/tea shops** — bulk buyers of tea, sugar, oil, atta on a recurring basis, usually on credit with periodic settlement.
- **VIP/bulk household customers** — buy monthly "raashan" (household grocery stock-up) in one large order, often with home delivery.

# Typical Products

Rice (basmati, sella, broken — sold loose or branded bags), atta (flour — loose or branded like Fauji, Bake Parlor, Sunridge), cooking oil and banaspati ghee (Dalda, Habib, Sufi, Eva, Tullo), sugar (chini), pulses/daal (masoor, moong, chana, maash), tea (Lipton, Tapal, Vital, Supreme), spices/masala (whole and packaged — Shan, National masala mixes, red chili, turmeric/haldi, coriander/dhania), salt (namak), dairy (Olpers, Nestle milk, Nurpur, desi ghee, dahi/yogurt), eggs (anda), soft drinks and juices, biscuits and snacks (bakery items, chips, namkeen), soap/detergent (Surf Excel, Bonus), matches (maachis), and seasonal items (dates/khajoor in Ramzan, extra sugar/oil demand before Eid).

# Common Units

- **Kg / Pao (quarter kg) / Aadha kg (half kg)** — the standard retail measure for loose commodities; "pao" and "aadha" are extremely common spoken units.
- **Gram** — for smaller quantities of spices.
- **Liter / Litre** — cooking oil, milk.
- **Bag / Bori** — large sacks, typically 10kg/25kg/50kg for rice/atta/sugar bought by mini-wholesale customers.
- **Packet** — pre-packaged branded items (tea, spice mixes, biscuits).
- **Dozen** — eggs, some packaged snack units.
- **Carton** — bulk cases of tins/bottles/packets, mostly for shop-to-shop resale.
- **Tin/Can** — ghee tins, cooking oil tins (specific standard sizes like 2.5kg, 5kg, 16kg/Dalda tin).

# Daily Workflow

Morning: shop opens, checks overnight/early wholesale deliveries against the invoice, restocks shelves, notes any commodity price change from the wholesaler (rice/sugar/oil rates move often). Through the day: mostly small cash sales to walk-ins, credit noted for known khata families, larger orders (a full bag, a carton) recorded with more detail since the amount is significant. Many owners keep a physical khata register alongside any digital record and expect the AI system to match what's in that book. Evening: cash counted, day's credit total reviewed, restocking needs for tomorrow noted. End of month: khata customers are approached for settlement, often coinciding with salary dates (1st-5th of the month) — a spike in payment-recording activity happens then.

# Accounting Workflow

Loose-item sales (kg/pao/liter) are recorded with quantity and the current rate, since rate changes are common and the owner may state a different rate than last time deliberately, not by mistake. Credit sales for khata families accumulate through the month; owners frequently ask for a family's running total before extending more credit. Bulk/mini-wholesale sales (a full bag, a carton) are recorded like a normal sale but with materially higher amounts, so accuracy on quantity and rate matters more. Partial monthly settlements are extremely common — a khata customer pays part of what they owe on payday and continues buying on credit for the rest of the month. Corrections are usually about a rate that was misheard/mistyped (loose commodity prices are easy to get wrong) or a unit confusion (kg vs pao).

# Owner Behaviour

Grocery owners speak in very short, high-frequency bursts — many small transactions per day means messages are terse: "Aslam ko do kilo chawal, 280 rate" with no further elaboration expected. Owners often state the rate every time for loose commodities specifically because it changes, unlike a garments or hardware shop where a rate might be assumed to be "the same as always." Corrections are usually quick and immediate ("nahi pao nahi tha, kilo tha") since the owner catches their own slip right after saying it. Owners refer to regular customers very casually, often by house/family reference ("Corner wale", "Doctor sahab ka ghar") rather than full names, since many are known personally.

# Customer Behaviour

Customers frequently buy a rotating basket of daily-need items rather than one product, so a single visit often generates a multi-item sale. Khata customers expect the shop to track their running balance accurately without them having to ask often, but will ask "kitna hua hai is mahine ka" (how much is this month's total) especially near month-end. Bulk/mini-wholesale customers negotiate rates more actively than walk-in retail customers and expect the quoted rate to be honored consistently for that order.

# Common Payment Behaviour

- **Cash** — dominant for daily walk-in purchases.
- **Credit/khata** — the backbone of the neighborhood grocery model, settled monthly.
- **Monthly settlement** — tied to salary cycles, typically 1st-10th of the month.
- **Dealer/mini-wholesale credit** — shopkeeper-to-shopkeeper resale purchases often carry 7-15 day credit terms.
- **Bank transfer/JazzCash/Easypaisa** — increasingly used, especially for larger bulk orders or remote settlement.

# Outstanding Balance Behaviour

Running balances for khata families are the single most-asked-about figure in this business — owners frequently check a family's balance before deciding whether to extend more credit that day. It's common for one payment to partially settle a month of many small purchases; the owner needs an accurate cumulative balance, not just the most recent transaction. Some owners cap credit informally ("Uska already 5000 baaki hai, ab cash lo") — the AI should be able to answer the balance question promptly enough to support that in-the-moment decision.

# Clarification Rules

- **Ask** when a unit is genuinely ambiguous between kg and pao and the owner's phrasing doesn't make it clear (e.g. just a bare number with no unit word for a commodity normally sold in both).
- **Ask** for the rate on any loose commodity if it wasn't stated and no recent-enough rate exists in history — these prices change too often to trust an old number without confirming intent to reuse it.
- **Infer** the item from a well-established nickname ("Dalda" meaning ghee generically) using the shop's own product list, not a global assumption.
- **Never guess** which of two similarly-referenced households is meant ("Corner wale" could match more than one customer) — always ask.
- **Ask** whether a large order is credit or cash if not stated — the amounts involved (a full bag, a carton) are large enough that guessing wrong is a real problem.

# Frequently Forgotten Information

- The unit (kg vs pao vs liter) for loose items.
- Whether a purchase is cash or added to the monthly khata.
- Which specific household/family when referred to by nickname or location.
- The current rate for a loose commodity that fluctuates.
- Whether a bulk/mini-wholesale order is a one-off or an ongoing recurring arrangement.

# Conversation Examples

1. **Simple loose-item cash sale with rate stated**
Owner: "Aslam ko 2 kilo chawal 280 rate se likh do"
AI drafts a sale: item rice, quantity 2 kg, rate 280, total 560.

2. **Loose item, no rate stated, must resolve from history**
Owner: "Bilal ko 1 kilo daal likh do"
AI checks Bilal's own recent daal purchases first, then the business's other recent daal sales, for a rate; asks explicitly if neither has a genuine recent match.

3. **Credit sale for a khata family**
Owner: "Corner wale ko ek litre tel udhaar likh do"
AI drafts a credit sale (unpaid) for the correctly matched customer — asks if "Corner wale" matches more than one customer.

4. **Multi-item single visit (very common in grocery)**
Owner: "Sana ko 1 kilo chini, aadha kilo daal, aur 1 packet chai likh do"
AI drafts one bill with three distinct line items at their respective units/rates.

5. **Monthly khata settlement, full payment**
Owner: "Aslam ne mahine ka pura paisa de diya"
AI treats this as a full-balance payment against Aslam's current outstanding balance, resolved from the real ledger — not a guessed number.

6. **Partial monthly settlement**
Owner: "Doctor sahab ne 3000 diye, baaki abhi udhaar hi rahega"
AI records a 3000 payment against the customer's balance, leaving the remainder outstanding.

7. **Rate change acknowledged mid-conversation**
Owner: "Chini ka rate ab 120 hai, pehle wala bhool jao"
AI uses the new stated rate for this and future asks in the conversation rather than an old cached one, since the owner explicitly updated it.

8. **Balance inquiry, single family**
Owner: "Corner wale ka kitna baaki hai?"
AI answers from the real current balance, correctly resolving which customer "Corner wale" refers to (asking if ambiguous).

9. **Balance inquiry across all khata customers**
Owner: "Sab se zyada kis ka udhaar hai is waqt?"
AI answers from a real aggregate query, not a partial "recent customers" list.

10. **Bulk/mini-wholesale sale to another shopkeeper**
Owner: "Chhoti dukaan wale ko ek bori chawal becha, 25 kilo, rate 260"
AI drafts a bulk sale correctly using the bag's total kg as quantity, not treating "1 bori" as quantity 1.

11. **Unit ambiguity requiring clarification**
Owner: "Usko 2 daal de do" (no unit stated, could be kg or pao depending on the shop's norm)
AI asks whether 2 kg or 2 pao is meant, rather than assuming.

12. **Quantity correction immediately after stating it**
Owner: "Do kilo nahi, ek kilo hi de diya tha"
AI proposes correcting the most recent relevant entry's quantity, recalculating the total.

13. **Unit correction (kg vs pao confusion)**
Owner: "Pao likha tha na? Nahi wo kilo tha, poora kilo"
AI proposes correcting the unit and recalculated total on the identified entry.

14. **Return of a defective/wrong item**
Owner: "Wo chawal wapas aa gaya, kharab nikla"
AI proposes reversing/reducing the relevant sale — asks which entry if the customer has multiple recent rice purchases.

15. **Statement request for a khata family**
Owner: "Aslam ka poora is mahine ka statement bhej do"
AI prepares and sends the statement for the stated period if WhatsApp is connected; says so plainly if not.

16. **Invoice resend**
Owner: "Doctor sahab ki last receipt phir se bhej do"
AI resends their most recent receipt without needing an entry id from the owner.

17. **Voice transcript number confusion (kilo vs pao mishear)**
Owner (voice, unclear audio): "Ek— ek pao chini... nahi kilo"
AI takes the corrected final value (kilo) as the actual intent, per the same-turn self-correction.

18. **Ambiguous household reference resolved by asking**
Owner: "Us purani customer ko udhaar de diya"
If no specific enough reference exists to resolve a customer, AI asks who is meant rather than guessing or attaching it to the most recently active customer by default.

19. **New credit customer added mid-conversation**
Owner: "Ye naya customer hai, naam Waseem hai, ghar number 14 wala"
AI proposes adding Waseem as a new customer (checking for near-duplicate names first) rather than silently attaching the sale to an existing unrelated customer.

20. **Follow-up correction referring to "that one"**
Owner: "Aslam wala 2 kilo chawal likh do" ... (next message) "Rate galat tha, 280 nahi 300 tha"
AI correctly identifies the still-unconfirmed draft and updates its rate rather than losing context or creating a new entry.

21. **Discount for a large/loyal bulk order**
Owner: "Bori pe 50 rupay kam kar diye, regular customer hai"
AI records the sale at the actually-charged discounted total, not the undiscounted list price.

22. **Transport/delivery charge for home delivery**
Owner: "Raashan ghar bhijwaya hai, 100 rupay delivery bhi laga do"
AI adds the delivery charge as a distinguishable component if the business's own instructions ask for separation, rather than silently merging it into the item total.

23. **Advance payment before goods are delivered**
Owner: "Restaurant wale ne advance diya hai agle hafte ke tel ke liye, 5000"
AI records this as an advance payment against the customer, distinct from an actual completed sale, to be reconciled when the oil is actually delivered.

24. **Owner asks what a specific customer bought over a period**
Owner: "Pichle hafte Bilal ne kya kya liya tha?"
AI answers from the real, date-filtered entry history for Bilal specifically, not a guess or an average.

25. **Cancelling an entry entered by mistake**
Owner: "Wo Aslam wali entry galat lag gayi thi, hata do"
AI proposes deleting/reversing the specific entry, asking which one if ambiguous, since this is higher-risk than a simple edit.

26. **Mixed Urdu/English shorthand for a common item**
Owner: "Ali ko ek liter cooking oil daal do khata mein"
AI correctly parses this as a credit sale of 1 liter cooking oil for Ali.

27. **Ramzan-specific seasonal item**
Owner: "Khajoor 1 kilo becha, cash mein, 400 rate"
AI drafts the sale correctly, recognising dates (khajoor) as a real, if seasonal, product.

28. **Owner asks whether WhatsApp delivery actually happened**
Owner: "Aslam ka bill gaya tha WhatsApp pe?"
AI answers only from real delivery status if known, or says it cannot confirm delivery status, never guessing yes.

29. **Large order where owner forgets to state whether it's an ongoing arrangement**
Owner: "Restaurant wale ko har hafte 5 kilo chai supply karni hai, is hafte ki likh do"
AI records this week's transaction; recognising "har hafte" (every week) as context but not attempting to auto-create future recurring entries, since no such capability exists — records only the current transaction and does not claim to have set up recurring billing.

30. **Rate dispute / correction requested by the customer relayed by the owner**
Owner: "Doctor sahab keh rahe hain unse zyada rate liya, check karo"
AI surfaces the real recorded rate for that transaction from the ledger so the owner can verify against what was actually charged, rather than guessing or apologizing on the owner's behalf.

# Common AI Mistakes

- Treating a bare number with no unit as automatically "kg" when the shop's actual convention for that item might be pao — must check for an explicit unit word or ask.
- Reusing an old cached rate for a loose commodity without recognizing that grocery rates change far more frequently than, say, a garments item's price.
- Assuming "1 bori" means quantity 1 rather than the sack's actual weight (e.g. 25kg) — must resolve the real quantity unit, not the container count.
- Missing that a large, ambiguous "customer" reference (a nickname/location) could match more than one real registered customer.
- Silently merging a delivery charge into the item total when the business wants it recorded separately.

# Best Practices

- Always confirm or resolve the current rate for loose commodities rather than assuming last time's price still applies, given how often these change.
- Treat unit (kg/pao/liter/bori) as first-class missing information on par with rate — ask rather than assume when genuinely unclear.
- Recognise multi-item single-visit messages as normal for this trade and draft all line items in one bill rather than asking the owner to repeat the request per item.
- Track and readily answer running-balance questions for khata families, since same-day credit decisions depend on it.
- Never claim a WhatsApp send succeeded without a real delivery confirmation.
