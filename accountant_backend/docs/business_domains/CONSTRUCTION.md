# Business Overview

A Pakistani construction material supplier sells cement, steel/sariya, sand (rait), bajri (crush/gravel), bricks (eent), blocks, and related raw building material to contractors (theykedar), builders, and individual house-builders. Shops range from a small mohalla-level cement/sariya dealer to a large yard supplying multiple active sites. Most business runs on contractor credit accounts settled against project milestones, not walk-in cash — a single contractor may have running balances against 3-4 active sites simultaneously, and the shop must track which delivery belongs to which site, not just which contractor. Steel is priced per-kg or per-maund and fluctuates daily with international scrap/billet prices, so the shop owner re-checks the day's rate before quoting; cement price is comparatively stable but varies by brand and by whether the order is a full truck or a partial lot. Delivery logistics (truck/mazda/suzuki) are a core part of the business — many entries are as much about "kis site pe jana hai" (which site to deliver to) as about the item itself. Off-book/cash "bina bill" sales are common for small individual buyers, while contractor accounts are almost always credit, tracked meticulously because amounts are large.

# Typical Customers

- **Contractors (Theykedar)** — the dominant customer type; multiple concurrent site accounts, large recurring credit, monthly or milestone-based settlement.
- **Builders/Developers** — bulk, scheduled deliveries tied to a construction phase (foundation, structure, finishing).
- **Individual house-builders** — building their own home, usually smaller quantities, often cash or short-term credit, price-sensitive and comparison-shopping.
- **Sub-contractors** (mistri, raj mistri crews) — sometimes buy directly on the main contractor's account, sometimes on their own small account.
- **Walk-in/one-off buyers** — repair jobs, small patch work, boundary walls — mostly cash.
- **Regular dealers/retailers** (for a wholesale-tier supplier) — smaller hardware/cement shops buying in bulk to resell.

# Typical Products

- Cement: Lucky Cement, Bestway Cement, DG Cement, Fauji Cement, Cherat Cement, Attock Cement, Maple Leaf Cement (bag and truck quantities)
- Steel/Sariya: Grade 60 deformed bars in sizes like 3/8", 1/2", 5/8", 3/4", 1" — brands like Amreli Steels, Agha Steel, Mughal Steel, Ittehad Steel
- Sand (Rait) — local/Chenab/Lawrencepur sand grades
- Bajri/Crush — different crush sizes (1 number, 2 number crush)
- Bricks (Eent) — awal (first-grade), doyam (second-grade), local kiln bricks
- Concrete blocks, hollow blocks
- Wire mesh, binding wire (tar)
- Waterproofing chemicals, curing compound
- Shuttering material (ply, bali/wooden beams) — rental item in many yards, not sold outright

# Common Units

- Bag (bori) — cement, 50kg standard bag
- Truck load / trolley — sand, bajri, bricks (a "truck" or "10-wheeler" of sand)
- Ton / Maund — steel, sometimes cement in bulk
- Kg — steel (per-kg rate is common alongside per-maund)
- Piece / 1000-piece lot — bricks
- Number (crush grading, e.g. "1 number bajri")
- Foot/Running foot — for certain fabricated steel items
- Rft (running feet) — for wire mesh rolls

# Daily Workflow

Morning: check today's steel rate (often phoned in or checked via a dealer WhatsApp group before quoting anyone), review pending truck deliveries scheduled for active sites. Through the day: contractors call or visit to place orders ("kal subah 2 truck bajri bhej dena site pe"), yard staff load trucks, delivery confirmed by phone once material reaches site. Credit is recorded against the specific site/contractor account as material leaves the yard, not when cash changes hands. Cash customers pay on the spot for small quantities. Evening: reconcile which trucks went out, which are still pending, follow up on any contractor who was supposed to pay against last week's bill. End of day: tally cash drawer against the day's cash sales; credit sales are just recorded, not reconciled to cash.

# Accounting Workflow

- **Sales**: recorded per delivery/truck, tied to a specific site name if the contractor runs multiple sites (critical — a contractor's total balance is meaningless to him without knowing which site it's for).
- **Credit**: the default for contractors; often no formal invoice at delivery time, just a running tally, with a proper bill generated only when the contractor asks for one (for their own client billing).
- **Cash**: small individual buyers, immediate settlement.
- **Advance**: contractors sometimes pay an advance against future deliveries for a project — must be recorded as a credit balance, not a sale.
- **Partial payments**: extremely common — a contractor pays whatever cash flow allows this week against a much larger running bill.
- **Outstanding balances**: tracked per contractor, ideally per site; large amounts (lakhs) are normal and not alarming the way they would be in a small retail shop.
- **Returns**: unused/excess material sometimes returned from a site (e.g. bricks not used) — credited back, often at a lower rate than purchase (breakage/handling).
- **Corrections**: quantity corrections are common when a truck is under/overloaded relative to what was verbally ordered; owner adjusts after the driver confirms actual delivered quantity.

# Owner Behaviour

Construction suppliers often speak in shorthand assuming shared context ("wohi wala site", "pichli dafa wala rate") because the same contractors call repeatedly. Owners frequently omit the site name when there's only one active job with that contractor, but must be reminded/asked when the contractor has multiple. Rates are quoted verbally per call and can differ from the previous week without the owner explicitly flagging it — corrections like "nahi wo purana rate tha, aaj ka alag hai" are common. Owners refer to contractors by name or by their site's nickname/location ("Bahria wala theykedar", "DHA site wala"). Mistakes are corrected quickly and matter-of-factly given the large sums involved — "us truck ki quantity ghalat likhi thi, 500 bag nahi 450 thi."

# Customer Behaviour

Contractors negotiate rates hard, especially on steel given daily price volatility, and expect the shop to remember standing arrangements without re-explaining. They often order by phone with minimal detail ("subah bhej dena, wohi jitna pichli dafa gaya tha") relying on the shop's own records rather than restating quantities. Payment follow-ups from the shop side ("bhai wo purana bill clear kar dein") are routine and expected, not awkward, given how normalized large running balances are in this trade.

# Common Payment Behaviour

- **Cash**: small/individual buyers, immediate.
- **Credit**: default for contractors, settled in installments over the life of the project or monthly.
- **Monthly settlement**: some contractors settle against a running account monthly regardless of individual delivery dates.
- **Advance against project**: paid at project start to secure priority/lock a rate before steel prices move.
- **Dealer/wholesale accounts**: smaller retail shops buying from a bigger yard, similarly on running credit with periodic settlement.

# Outstanding Balance Behaviour

Contractor balances are large and expected to stay outstanding for weeks or months — this is normal, not a red flag, and must not be treated as "the customer is in trouble." The shop tracks balances per contractor and, where the contractor runs multiple sites, per site, since payment against one site's bill should not silently offset another site's balance unless the contractor explicitly says to move it. Balances are chased actively when a project appears to be wrapping up (last delivery for that site already happened).

# Clarification Rules

- If a contractor has more than one active site, the AI MUST ask which site a new sale/delivery belongs to before drafting it — never guess based on the most recent site.
- If a steel rate isn't stated and the owner hasn't mentioned "aaj ka rate", the AI should check this business's own recent sales of that steel size for the most recently charged rate (per the item-price resolution mechanism), and if none exists, ask rather than guess — steel rates move daily and an old rate could be significantly wrong.
- If "truck" or "trolley" is used without a stated quantity, ask for the actual quantity (bags/tons) — truck capacity varies and should never be assumed.
- Never assume a return/credit-back rate matches the original sale rate without the owner stating it — breakage/handling deductions are common and must be confirmed.

# Frequently Forgotten Information

- Which site a delivery is for, when a contractor has multiple active jobs
- The current day's steel rate (assumed as "obvious" by the owner since he just checked it)
- Whether a delivery was a full truck or partial load
- Whether an advance is against a specific project phase or the account generally

# Conversation Examples

1. **Cash sale, small buyer**: "5 bag Lucky cement cash mein de do, ghar banwa raha hoon" → AI drafts a cash sale, no customer credit balance change since payment received equals total.
2. **Credit sale tied to a site**: "Bahria site pe 2 truck bajri bhej do theykedar Aslam ke naam" → AI checks if Aslam has multiple sites on record; if only one, drafts against it; if more than one, asks which site.
3. **Credit sale, contractor with multiple sites, ambiguous**: "Aslam ko 500 bag cement likh do" → Aslam has both "Bahria site" and "DHA site" accounts → AI must ask which site before drafting.
4. **Partial payment against a large balance**: "Aslam ne 2 lakh diye apne purane bill mein se" → AI records a partial payment reducing Aslam's balance, does not close the account.
5. **Full settlement of a completed site**: "DHA wala site khatam ho gaya, Aslam ne poora paisa de diya" → AI records a full-balance payment for that specific site/account (using the full-balance mechanism), confirms the amount before writing it if the exact figure wasn't stated.
6. **Advance payment before delivery**: "Aslam ne naye site ke liye 5 lakh advance diya hai, abhi maal nahi gaya" → AI records this as a credit/advance on the account, not tied to a sale, since no delivery happened yet.
7. **Steel rate not stated, resolved from history**: "3 ton sariya half inch Kareem ko bhej do" (no rate given) → AI checks Kareem's/business's recent sales of half-inch sariya for the last charged rate and uses it, clearly showing the rate on the draft for owner confirmation.
8. **Steel rate volatility — rate stated explicitly overrides history**: "aaj rate 285 hai per kg, Kareem ko 2 ton bhej do isi rate pe" → AI uses the stated rate, not any historical one, since the owner explicitly gave today's rate.
9. **Truck quantity ambiguous**: "1 truck bajri bhej do Farooq ko" (no bag/ton count) → AI asks how many tons/loads count as this truck, since truck capacity isn't standardized.
10. **Multiple products in one message**: "Farooq ko 200 bag cement, 3 truck bajri aur 1000 eent likh do" → AI drafts a single bill with three line items.
11. **Correction after delivery — quantity was wrong**: "wo truck ki entry ghalat hai, 500 bag nahi thi, 450 thi" → AI proposes an edit to the specific recent entry, asking to confirm if more than one recent truck delivery could match.
12. **Return of unused material**: "site se 50 bag cement wapis aayi hai, Aslam ke account mein credit kar do" → AI drafts a return/credit at the same rate unless the owner states a different rate, and explicitly asks if breakage/handling should reduce the credited rate.
13. **Discount for a large/regular contractor**: "Aslam ko is dafa 5 rupay kam rate pe de do, bara order hai" → AI applies the stated discount to the drafted rate, does not apply it to any other pending item unless told.
14. **Transport charge as a separate line**: "maal ki qeemat alag, transport 5000 alag likh do" → AI records transport as a distinct line item/charge, not folded into material cost.
15. **Balance inquiry, single site**: "Farooq ka kitna baaki hai" → AI answers with the real current_balance from the ledger for Farooq (if he has one active site) or asks which site if he has more than one.
16. **Balance inquiry across multiple sites**: "Aslam ke dono site ka total kitna hai" → AI sums balances across Aslam's linked accounts/sites and states each individually plus the total.
17. **Statement request for a contractor**: "Aslam ka pichle mahine ka poora hisab bhej do" → AI prepares a statement for the prior calendar month and, if WhatsApp is connected, sends it; otherwise says so plainly.
18. **Invoice/bilti request**: "iss delivery ki pakki rasid banao" → AI generates a formal invoice for the specific recent delivery.
19. **Voice transcript error — number/word confusion**: voice input transcribes "panch sau bag" as "panch so bag" — AI still correctly reads it as 500 bags from context, not "so" as a separate word.
20. **Roman Urdu spelling variation**: "2 truck bajary Farooq ko bej do" (bajary=bajri, bej=bhej) — AI correctly interprets both misspellings.
21. **Mixed Urdu/English**: "Aslam ka outstanding balance kitna hai, total?" → AI answers in the business's chosen reply language with the real balance.
22. **Ambiguous item — "sariya" without size**: "2 ton sariya bhej do Kareem ko" → since sariya comes in multiple sizes, AI checks Kareem's own recent purchases for a single matching size; if he's bought multiple sizes before, AI asks which size rather than guessing.
23. **Follow-up question after a draft**: owner says "wo sahi hai, bhej do" after being shown a draft in the previous turn → AI treats it as confirmation of the most recent unconfirmed draft, not a new request.
24. **Cancelling an entry**: "kal wali cement wali entry hatao, wo order cancel ho gaya tha" → AI proposes deleting/reversing the specific matching entry, asking for confirmation if more than one entry from that day could match.
25. **Dealer/wholesale account, not a contractor**: "chhoti dukaan wale Rashid ko 100 bag cement wholesale rate pe de do" → AI applies whatever wholesale rate context/history indicates for Rashid specifically, distinct from retail/contractor rates.
26. **Site-specific edge case — material moved between sites**: "Bahria site ka 50 bag DHA site pe bhej diya, udhar credit kar do idhar se" → AI proposes moving/crediting the specific quantity from one site-account to another, confirming both accounts before applying.
27. **Rate negotiated mid-conversation**: owner first says "285 per kg" then corrects "nahi 280 kar do usko" before confirming → AI uses the corrected, most recent rate in the final draft.
28. **Large round-number credit sale with no items specified beyond "sariya"**: "Kareem ko 5 lakh ka sariya likh do" → since only a total is given with no quantity/size, AI asks what size and how many tons/kg, since a bare total can't be split into a real line item.
29. **Advance offsetting a future delivery**: "Aslam ka advance se 2 truck bajri ki value kaat lo" → AI applies the delivery against the existing advance credit rather than creating new debt, reducing the advance balance accordingly.
30. **End-of-project full reconciliation**: "Bahria site poora ho gaya, Aslam ka is site ka pura hisab final kar do, jo bhi baaki hai wo bata do" → AI reports the final outstanding balance for that specific site before any payment is recorded, since the owner is asking for the number first, not requesting an action yet.

# Common AI Mistakes

- Applying a payment or new sale to the wrong site when a contractor has multiple active site accounts.
- Assuming an old/cached steel rate is still valid without checking whether the owner stated today's rate explicitly — steel prices move daily and this is a real financial risk.
- Treating "1 truck" as a fixed, known quantity instead of asking, since truck capacity is not standardized in this trade.
- Applying a return/credit at the original sale rate without confirming — breakage/handling deductions are common.
- Treating a large outstanding balance as unusual or alarming — it is completely normal in this trade and must not trigger unnecessary clarification or hesitation.

# Best Practices

- Always confirm which site a transaction belongs to whenever a contractor has more than one active account.
- Always surface the resolved rate (whether from context, history, or the owner's own statement) clearly on the draft before confirmation, given how large the sums involved typically are.
- Treat advances and delivery-based sales as distinct ledger events — never conflate an advance payment with revenue from a specific delivery unless the owner explicitly links them.
- When in doubt about quantity, size, or site, ask — the size of a typical transaction in this trade makes a wrong guess far more costly than in a small retail shop.
