# Business Overview

A Pakistani restaurant or dhaba ranges from a small roadside dhaba with 4-5 tables to a mid-size
family restaurant with AC seating, delivery riders, and a separate cash counter. Most are
family-run or run by a trusted manager (munshi) who the owner checks in with daily or weekly.
Cash is still the dominant payment method for walk-in customers; credit ("khata") is common only
for two groups — regular office-goers/employees from nearby shops who eat daily and settle
monthly, and known local families who order frequently. Raw material (meat, vegetables, dairy,
flour, cooking oil) is usually bought from a mandi or a fixed supplier on daily or weekly credit,
settled weekly or monthly — the restaurant is simultaneously a debtor (to suppliers) and a
creditor (to regular eating customers), and owners often mentally track both sides even though
this app is usually used for the customer/sales side. Business is heavily driven by mealtimes
(lunch and dinner rush), Ramadan (iftar/sehri volume spikes dramatically), and wedding/party
season (bulk catering orders). Delivery via riders (own staff or Foodpanda-style platforms) has
grown fast in urban areas; platform commission and payment settlement lag is a real operational
detail owners track separately from in-house cash.

# Typical Customers

- **Walk-in / table customers** — pay cash on the spot, rarely tracked as a "customer" record at
  all unless they run a tab during the meal.
- **Regular/daily customers** — nearby shopkeepers, office staff, students who eat lunch/dinner
  daily and settle at week's or month's end ("mahine ke akhir mein hisaab").
- **Credit/khata customers** — known families or individuals the owner trusts, ordering on phone
  or in person, settled periodically.
- **Delivery/online order customers** — via rider or platform; usually prepaid or cash-on-delivery,
  rarely credit.
- **Corporate/bulk order customers** — offices ordering lunch boxes regularly, often on a monthly
  invoice basis.
- **Wedding/function/catering customers** — large one-off orders, usually advance-paid partially,
  balance on the day or after.
- **Staff meals** — technically a cost, not a sale, but owners sometimes mention it in the same
  breath as customer orders ("staff ka khana alag rakho").

# Typical Products

Karahi (chicken/mutton), BBQ items (seekh kabab, tikka, boti, malai boti), Biryani, Pulao, Nihari,
Haleem, Daal (chana/mash/masoor), Sabzi (aloo gosht, bhindi, karela), Roti/Naan/Paratha, Raita,
Salad, Cold drinks (Pepsi/Coke/Sprite bottles and cans), Lassi, Doodh Patti chai, Kashmiri chai,
Fresh juice, Zarda/Kheer/Gulab Jamun (desserts), Chicken/Mutton/Beef by weight for karahi pricing,
Family packs, Lunch boxes for offices, Wedding/function catering packages (per-head pricing).

# Common Units

- **Plate** — the most common restaurant unit; "1 plate biryani", "2 plate karahi".
- **Kg** — meat/chicken sold/costed by weight for karahi ("1 kg chicken karahi").
- **Piece** — roti/naan/paratha counted individually.
- **Dozen** — sometimes for roti orders in bulk ("2 dozen roti").
- **Per head / per plate** — for wedding/function catering pricing.
- **Family pack / half / full** — portion-size units specific to this trade (biryani half/full,
  karahi half/full — "half" usually means enough for 2-3 people).
- **Bottle / can** — cold drinks.
- **Glass** — lassi, juice, chai.

# Daily Workflow

Morning prep (marinating meat, prepping vegetables, dough for roti) begins before opening.
Lunch rush is the first big sales window; the manager or owner reconciles cash at a lull
mid-afternoon. Evening/dinner rush is usually the bigger of the two. Delivery orders are logged
separately (often on a rider's own slip/app) but the cash/online payment still needs to land in
the day's total. At closing, cash counted against the day's chits/orders, credit customers'
running tabs updated, supplier payments due that day settled if applicable, and the till balanced
before the next day starts. Weekly: supplier credit settled. Monthly: regular customer/khata
accounts settled and corporate lunch invoices sent out.

# Accounting Workflow

- **Cash sale**: recorded as a straightforward sale, usually per table/order, sometimes per item
  if the owner wants item-level detail (rare for very small dhabas, common for slightly bigger
  ones tracking food cost).
- **Credit/khata sale**: recorded against the regular customer's running balance, settled weekly
  or monthly, not per-meal.
- **Advance/deposit for catering**: recorded as an advance payment against a future large order —
  the balance is billed once the event happens.
- **Partial payment**: common for large catering/wedding orders — "aadha abhi, aadha function
  wale din."
- **Corrections**: an order total often gets corrected right after billing because a rider mixed
  up items or a table's order was mis-added — owners expect quick correction, not a formal
  refund/return process.
- **Returns**: rare for food (can't "return" cooked food), but a wrong/spoiled item leads to a
  discount or a comp ("wo item maaf kar do") rather than a literal return entry.

# Owner Behaviour

Restaurant owners/managers talk in short, transactional bursts — "2 karahi, 1 family biryani,
parcel" — with almost no explicit pricing spoken aloud during the rush; pricing is assumed known
from a fixed or seasonal menu unless it's a custom/bulk order. Corrections happen fast and
verbally right after an order is placed ("nahi nahi, ek karahi nahi do karahi"). Owners refer to
regular customers by name or by shop/office ("saamne wali dukaan wala", "office wale sahab"), and
to catering clients by event ("Ahmed sahab ki shaadi wala order"). At month-end, owners ask for
consolidated totals per regular customer rather than per-meal detail.

# Customer Behaviour

Walk-in customers rarely discuss money beyond paying the bill. Regular/khata customers often just
say "likh do" (write it down) without stating an amount, trusting the standard price. Catering
clients negotiate per-head pricing explicitly and often want a written estimate before committing
advance payment.

# Common Payment Behaviour

Cash is dominant for walk-in and delivery. Credit/khata is extended selectively to known regulars
and settled weekly (daily-eating office staff) or monthly. Catering/function orders are typically
30-50% advance, balance on/after the event day. Supplier payments (meat, vegetable mandi) are
often daily-cash or short-term weekly credit — this is a cost the owner tracks, not typically a
"customer" in this app, but owners sometimes mention it in the same conversation ("mandi wale ko
bhi paisay dene hain") and the AI should recognize that's a different kind of obligation, not a
customer sale.

# Outstanding Balance Behaviour

Regular/khata customers' balances are usually small and settled predictably (weekly payday,
month-end). Large catering balances are the ones owners actively chase, since a wedding-sized
order represents real exposure if the remaining balance isn't collected promptly after the event.

# Clarification Rules

- If an order names a dish but the size/portion (half/full, per-head) is missing and it isn't
  clear from context or the customer's known pattern, ask rather than assume "full."
- If "likh do" is said with no amount and the customer/item combination has no clear recent
  precedent to infer a standard price from, ask for the amount rather than guess.
- Never assume a walk-in customer is a credit customer just because a name was mentioned in
  passing — only treat it as credit if the owner explicitly says so ("iska likh do", "udhaar").
- For catering/function orders, always confirm whether the stated amount is the advance or the
  full order value if it isn't stated explicitly — these are very different entries.
- Never invent a per-head catering rate; if none was stated and none exists in this business's
  own history for a similar order, ask.

# Frequently Forgotten Information

Owners frequently forget to state: whether an order is dine-in, parcel/takeaway, or delivery
(matters for whether a delivery charge applies); whether a stated amount for a catering order is
an advance or the full value; whether "half" or "full" portion was meant; whether a discount was
already applied verbally before the "likh do" instruction.

# Conversation Examples

1. **Simple cash table order**
Owner: "table 4, 2 karahi full, 3 roti, cash"
AI: Records a cash sale — 2 full karahi + 3 roti — no customer needed for a walk-in cash table order; confirms total based on standard menu pricing if known, or asks for the amount if this business hasn't priced these items before.

2. **Credit sale to a regular office customer, no amount stated**
Owner: "Ahmed ka lunch likh do"
AI: Looks up Ahmed's recent order pattern (same item/price as his usual daily lunch) to propose the entry; if no clear recent pattern exists, asks what today's lunch amount was rather than assuming yesterday's.

3. **Partial payment on a catering order**
Owner: "shaadi wale order ka 20000 advance mila hai, total 60000 tha"
AI: Records a 20,000 advance payment against a 60,000 total catering order for that customer, leaving 40,000 outstanding — not a full settlement.

4. **Full settlement of weekly khata**
Owner: "Ali ne is hafte ka pura hisaab clear kar diya"
AI: Treats this as a full-balance payment — proposes recording payment equal to Ali's current outstanding balance, not inventing a number, per the same "puri payment" handling used elsewhere in this system.

5. **Correction immediately after an order**
Owner: "nahi wo 2 karahi nahi thi, 1 hi thi"
AI: Recognizes this as a correction to the just-created entry (not a new order) and proposes editing the most recent draft/entry's quantity rather than creating a second, contradictory sale.

6. **Balance inquiry**
Owner: "Ahmed ka kitna baaki hai"
AI: Answers from the real current balance on record — never estimates or recalls from memory.

7. **Statement request for a regular customer**
Owner: "Ahmed ki is mahine ki statement bhej do"
AI: Prepares a statement for the current month's entries for Ahmed and queues it for WhatsApp delivery once confirmed, correctly stating if WhatsApp isn't connected rather than claiming it sent.

8. **Delivery order with delivery charge**
Owner: "1 family biryani delivery, 150 delivery charge alag se"
AI: Records the biryani sale and the delivery charge as a distinct line/amount, not silently folded into the food total, since the owner explicitly separated them.

9. **Multiple items, single customer, single message**
Owner: "Bilal ko 2 chicken tikka, 1 daal, 4 roti, 1 lassi likh do 1200 ka"
AI: Records all four items under Bilal with the stated total; if item-level rates aren't given and don't need to be (total stated), does not force item-level pricing unless this business tracks it that way.

10. **Discount applied verbally**
Owner: "regular customer hai, 100 discount de diya, likh do 900"
AI: Records the sale at the stated post-discount amount (900); does not need to separately model the discount unless the owner wants it itemized.

11. **Ambiguous "half" vs "full" not stated**
Owner: "1 karahi Bilal ko likh do"
AI: If Bilal has no recent pattern to infer portion size from and portion materially changes price, asks whether it's half or full rather than assuming full.

12. **Voice transcript with trailing/cut-off info**
Owner (voice): "do biryani ek karahi... parcel... uh Ahmed ke liye... hazar do sau"
AI: Reconstructs: 2 biryani + 1 karahi, parcel (takeaway), for Ahmed, total 1200 — asks for confirmation of ambiguous fragments only if the reconstruction is genuinely uncertain.

13. **Roman Urdu spelling variant for date**
Owner: "5 tarikh ka udhar dikhao Ahmed ka"
AI: Recognizes "tarikh" as a date-of-month reference (same as "tareek"/"tareekh") and resolves it to the 5th of the current or most recent applicable month.

14. **Mixed Urdu/English**
Owner: "Ahmed's lunch order likh do, 800 total, cash nahi credit hai"
AI: Records a credit (not cash) sale of 800 for Ahmed.

15. **Follow-up question after a draft**
Owner: "bill banao" → AI drafts → Owner: "isko WhatsApp pe bhej do"
AI: Understands "isko" refers to the just-drafted bill, not a new request, and proceeds to prepare it for sending rather than asking "which bill."

16. **Vague reference to "kal wali entry"**
Owner: "kal wali Ahmed ki entry hatao"
AI: Locates yesterday's entry for Ahmed specifically (not today's, not another customer's) and proposes deleting/reversing it, asking for confirmation if more than one entry for Ahmed exists on that date.

17. **Corporate lunch monthly invoice request**
Owner: "office wale customer ka mahine ka pura bill bhej do"
AI: Prepares a statement/invoice covering the full month for that corporate customer.

18. **Advance-only entry with no total yet known**
Owner: "shaadi ka bayana mila hai 15000, total abhi tay nahi hua"
AI: Records a 15,000 advance payment against that customer, without inventing a total order value that hasn't been agreed yet.

19. **Trade-specific edge case: comped/wasted item**
Owner: "ek karahi jal gaya tha, customer se paisay nahi liye, maaf kar diya"
AI: Recognizes this is not a sale to record (or is a zero-charge item) rather than a bill entry with a real amount — asks the owner how they want this reflected (e.g. not recorded at all, or recorded as waste/loss) rather than defaulting to logging it as revenue.

20. **Trade-specific edge case: staff meal, not a customer sale**
Owner: "staff ka khana bhi likh lena side mein"
AI: Recognizes staff meals are a cost, not a customer sale, and asks how the owner wants this tracked (this system's customer/sales model isn't built for staff-meal cost tracking) rather than creating a fake customer entry for it.

21. **Wrong customer's order corrected**
Owner: "wo order Ahmed ka nahi Bilal ka tha"
AI: Proposes transferring the just-created entry from Ahmed to Bilal rather than creating a duplicate entry for Bilal and leaving Ahmed's wrong one in place.

22. **Bulk roti order in dozens**
Owner: "5 dozen roti likh do function ke liye, 3000"
AI: Records 5 dozen (60 pieces) roti at a total of 3,000 for the stated customer/function.

23. **Per-head catering pricing stated**
Owner: "200 mehmaan, per head 800, function wala order"
AI: Computes and records a catering order for 200 guests at Rs. 800/head = Rs. 160,000 total, tying it to the correct customer/event.

24. **Owner asks what a specific customer bought on a specific date**
Owner: "Ahmed ne 5 tareek ko kya liya tha"
AI: Answers from real entries on/around the 5th for Ahmed — never invents an item/amount if nothing is on record for that date, and says so plainly if nothing is found.

25. **Undo a wrongly recorded discount**
Owner: "wo discount wapis le lo, poori qeemat legi"
AI: Proposes correcting the just-recorded discounted entry back to full price rather than adding a second offsetting entry.

26. **Ambiguous pronoun referring to which draft**
Owner: "ye bhi Ahmed ka hai" (after two different draft entries were just discussed)
AI: If it's genuinely unclear which of the two drafts "ye" refers to, asks rather than guessing which one to attach to Ahmed.

27. **Weekly settlement across multiple small credit customers**
Owner: "is hafte ke sab regular customers ka hisaab clear ho gaya"
AI: This is broad and ambiguous across multiple customers — asks the owner to confirm this means recording a full-balance payment for each regular/credit customer, or asks which specific customers, rather than silently guessing the scope.

# Common AI Mistakes

- Treating every walk-in cash order as if it needs a "customer" record — most don't.
- Assuming an advance payment equals the full order value for catering/function orders.
- Confusing a same-order correction ("nahi 2 nahi 1 thi") with a brand-new order.
- Silently folding a stated delivery charge into the food total instead of keeping it distinct
  when the owner separated them.
- Assuming "half"/"full" portion when it materially changes price and there's no reliable basis
  to infer it.
- Logging comped/wasted/staff-meal items as ordinary revenue-generating sales.

# Best Practices

Keep entries fast and low-friction for busy meal-rush dictation — don't demand item-level detail
this business doesn't normally track. Distinguish clearly between an advance and a full payment
on catering orders, since the exposure gap matters financially. Recognize same-order corrections
immediately after a draft rather than treating them as new orders. Never invent per-head or
per-plate pricing that wasn't stated and has no clear precedent — ask.
