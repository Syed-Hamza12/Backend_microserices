# Business Overview

A Pakistani hardware store ("hardware ki dukaan") is a neighborhood or bazaar-based shop selling
plumbing pipes and fittings, nails, screws, nuts and bolts, wire mesh, hinges, locks, hand tools,
GI/PVC pipe, taps, and — in most small-to-mid shops — electrical fittings (switches, wire, MCBs)
and sometimes paint/hardware combined under one roof, since a single shopkeeper often serves all
of a household's "banana/theek karna" needs. Shops range from a single-counter operation in a
mohalla market to a larger establishment on a hardware-specific street (e.g. parts of Old
Anarkali/McLeod Road in Lahore, or Bohri Bazaar-adjacent hardware clusters in Karachi) that also
supplies contractors and other retailers wholesale. Stock is bought from wholesalers/importers
(often in bulk gross/carton lots) and resold by the piece, foot, or kg. Most transactions are
still cash or credit-on-a-known-face; digital payment is rare except for regular dealer/contractor
accounts settled monthly by bank transfer. A hardware shop's ledger is dominated by running
udhaar (credit) accounts for known contractors, plumbers, and electricians who buy daily/weekly
and settle periodically, alongside walk-in cash sales for one-off household repairs.

# Typical Customers

- **Walk-in retail** — a homeowner buying a handful of nails or one tap, almost always cash.
- **Regular/credit customers** — local plumbers, electricians, and masons ("mistri") who buy
  small quantities constantly and settle weekly or monthly. This is the backbone of a hardware
  shop's udhaar book.
- **Contractors** — buy in bulk for a specific job (a house under construction), often on credit
  tied to project milestones, sometimes paying in installments as the client releases funds to
  them.
- **Other retailers (wholesale)** — smaller shops or hardware stalls buying from a bigger dealer
  at wholesale rate, in gross/carton quantities.
- **Factories/workshops** — buy specific recurring items (a certain gauge of wire, a particular
  bolt size) in bulk, usually on a monthly account.

# Typical Products

Pipes & fittings: GI pipe, PVC pipe, PPR pipe, elbow, tee, coupling, union, flange, reducer, nipple,
socket, plug, bend. Taps & sanitary hardware: bibcock, mixer tap, shower set, flush valve, angle
valve. Fasteners: nails (kaeel), screws (peench), nuts-bolts, washers, rawal plugs, anchor bolts.
Wire & mesh: chicken mesh, GI wire, binding wire, barbed wire, welded mesh. Hand tools: hammer,
plas (pliers), pana (spanner/wrench), screwdriver (peechkas), hacksaw, hand drill, measuring tape
(fita). Locks & hardware: padlock (tala), door hinges (kabza), door handles, tower bolt (chatkhani),
door closer. Electrical (often stocked alongside): wire (tar), switch, socket, MCB, holder, PVC
pipe/conduit for wiring, junction box. Miscellaneous: cement (sometimes small-bag retail), fevicol/
adhesive, silicone sealant, gum tape, sand paper.

# Common Units

- **Piece (pcs / adad)** — taps, hinges, locks, single tools.
- **Foot (feet / fut)** — pipe, wire mesh, sometimes rope/hose.
- **Kg** — nails, screws, wire (sold loose by weight very commonly), GI sheet.
- **Dozen (darjan)** — hinges, small hardware sold in dozens to shopkeepers/tradesmen.
- **Gross (12 dozen)** — bulk fastener purchases from wholesaler to retailer.
- **Bundle (bandal)** — GI wire coils, rebar-adjacent items.
- **Roll** — mesh, tape, wire on a spool.
- **Bag (bori/thaila)** — cement, sand-adhesive mixes.
- **Set** — a shower set, a lock set (body + key + strike plate).
- **Inch/mm** — used constantly for pipe diameter and screw/nail length ("1 inch nail", "20mm
  pipe", "3/4 inch tap").

# Daily Workflow

Morning: shop opens, previous day's cash is counted and any bank deposit made; supplier
deliveries (naya maal) are received and checked against the order, sometimes with a partial
advance already paid to the supplier. Mid-day: steady stream of walk-in and regular
plumber/electrician customers; owner or a shagird (helper) fetches items while the owner or
munshi (accounts clerk) notes down credit sales in a khata (ledger register) or now increasingly
in the app. Afternoon: contractor/bulk orders are packed for delivery, sometimes with a
labor/rider charge added. Evening: cash is recalculated against the day's khata entries; new
credit given during the day is reviewed; a regular customer may stop by specifically to "hisab
karna" (settle up) before evening prayers. Closing: cash drawer counted, register/app totals
compared, any discrepancy investigated immediately since credit given verbally is the shop's
single biggest risk.

# Accounting Workflow

Cash sales are recorded immediately, item by item when it matters (a contractor wants a
breakdown) or as a lump sum for small retail sales. Credit sales ("udhaar") are always tied to a
named, known customer — a hardware shop almost never gives credit to an unknown walk-in. Partial
payments are extremely common: a plumber buys pipe worth 8,000 but pays 3,000 now, "baaki baad
mein" (rest later) — this must be recorded as a sale of 8,000 with a payment of 3,000 against it,
not as a sale of 5,000. Advance payments happen when a contractor pre-pays for a bulk order to be
delivered later. Returns are common for wrong-size pipe/fittings and are usually a straight
quantity reversal at the same rate, not a discount. Corrections are frequent — an item's quantity
or rate is misheard or the wrong size was written down, and needs a clean edit of the specific
entry, not a new offsetting entry.

# Common Pakistani Vocabulary

Maal (goods/stock), farokht (sale), udhaar/udhar (credit), naqad (cash), advance/bayana,
wapsi (return), tabdeeli (exchange), rate, bhaav (price/rate, especially wholesale), hisaab

# Product Nicknames

"Char number tar" (a specific common gauge of GI wire), "adha inch pipe" (half-inch pipe, the
most common household size), "chota tala" / "bara tala" (small/large padlock by size rather than
brand), "peechkas" (screwdriver, from the Persian/Urdu loanword, extremely common shop word),
"pana" (any spanner/wrench, size implied by context or stated separately e.g. "10 number pana"),
"kaeel" (nails, often specified by weight/length: "2 kg kaeel do inch wale"), "chatkhani" (tower
bolt latch), "kabza" (hinge), "rawal plug" (wall anchor, said as one word "rawalplug"), "elbow"
and "tee" used in English even in otherwise all-Roman-Urdu sentences since there's no common Urdu
substitute in trade speech.

# Product Categories

Plumbing (pipes, fittings, taps, valves), Fasteners (nails, screws, nuts-bolts, anchors),
Tools (hand tools, measuring), Locks & Door Hardware, Wire & Mesh, Electrical (where stocked
alongside), Sealants & Adhesives, Miscellaneous/General Hardware.

# Owner Behaviour

Hardware shop owners speak in short, transactional bursts and assume the listener (traditionally
a munshi, now the AI) already knows their regular customers and standard rates — they will name
an item and quantity and expect the rate/customer to be inferred from history unless it's a new
item or a new customer, in which case they state it explicitly. They frequently give an
instruction mid-transaction while still serving the next customer, so messages can be clipped
("Ali ko 2 pipe, baaki kal"). Corrections are stated tersely and expect the AI to find the exact
recent entry, not to be walked through it step by step ("nahi 3 nahi thay, 2 thay" — no, it
wasn't 3, it was 2). Owners refer to customers by first name or by trade/relationship ("mistri
wala Sharif", "colony wala Aslam") more than by full formal name.

# Customer Behaviour

Regular tradesmen customers (plumbers, electricians) often buy multiple small items across a
single visit and expect it totaled as one entry unless they ask for a breakdown (needed if they
are billing their own client separately for materials). They frequently ask for their running
balance verbally before deciding whether to pay something now. Walk-in retail customers are
brief and usually pay cash on the spot with no back-and-forth. Contractors negotiate rate on bulk
orders and expect that negotiated rate to be remembered for the rest of that job, not
re-negotiated each visit.

# Common Payment Behaviour

Cash is dominant for retail; credit (udhaar) is dominant for tradesmen/contractors, usually
settled weekly (for a plumber buying daily) or at project completion (for a contractor). Dealer/
wholesale customers of a larger hardware shop often settle monthly by bank transfer or cheque.
Partial payment against a specific bulk purchase is routine and must be linked to that purchase,
not treated as a generic balance reduction, when the owner says something like "8000 ka pipe tha,
5000 de diye" (the pipe was 8000, they gave 5000).

# Outstanding Balance Behaviour

A hardware shop's owner tracks udhaar very closely because a single missed entry on a busy day
can mean real financial loss — this is the #1 reason "likh do" (write it down) instructions are
issued immediately, even mid-serving-another-customer. Owners frequently ask for a specific
customer's running balance before deciding whether to extend more credit that day ("Sharif ka
kitna baaki hai, aur maal de doon?"). Balances are chased verbally on a personal-relationship
basis, not through formal reminders — but owners want the app to know the exact number the moment
they ask.

# Clarification Rules

- If an item is named with a size/spec that has multiple real variants in this shop's own history
  (e.g., "pipe" when the shop sells 1/2", 3/4", and 1" pipe) and no size was given, ask which size
  — never assume the most common one.
- If a rate is missing and no matching item/rate exists anywhere in this shop's recorded history
  (own customer or others), ask for the rate explicitly rather than estimate from a "typical"
  hardware price, since rates vary by supplier cycle and negotiation.
- If a quantity is given in an ambiguous unit for that item (e.g., "5 wire" — kg? roll? feet?),
  ask which unit, since wire is sold multiple ways in the same shop.
- Never assume a partial-payment phrase means the full amount was received — "kuch de diye" (gave
  some) is explicitly partial and the exact figure must be asked for if not stated.
- If the same item name could plausibly mean two different products this shop stocks (e.g. "tape"
  — measuring tape vs. gum/sealant tape), ask rather than guess from context alone if context is
  genuinely ambiguous.

# Frequently Forgotten Information

Owners very often omit: which specific size/spec of a multi-variant item, whether a stated amount
is a payment against an existing balance or a new sale total, whether "wapas" means an entry
correction or an actual physical return of goods, and the date when back-recording a delayed
entry ("kal wali baat thi" — that was from yesterday — without saying today is a different date).

# Voice Examples

"Ali ko... [pause] ...do pipe likh do, aadha inch wale" (Write down two pipes for Ali, half-inch
ones — trailing spec added after a pause, common in dictation). "Sharif ne... paanch... nahi
saath... saath sau diye the" (Sharif gave five — no, six — six hundred — a self-correction
mid-sentence that voice transcription will render as spoken, including the false start). "Naya
customer... Bilal naam hai... number likh lo... zero three tree double one..." (digits spoken
individually, transcription may render as words "zero three three one" needing numeric
normalization). "Kitna baaki hai Aslam ka... colony wala" (balance query with an identifying
descriptor instead of a surname).

# Conversation Examples

**1. Simple cash sale, single item**
Owner: "Ek pana becha, 200 rupay."
AI records a cash sale of Rs. 200 for one wrench, no customer link needed since it's an anonymous
walk-in — asks only if the owner wants it tied to a specific customer record.

**2. Credit sale to a known regular, item + quantity, rate inferred from history**
Owner: "Sharif ko 5 pipe likh do adha inch wale."
AI checks Sharif's own recent purchases for "1/2 inch pipe", finds his last rate, drafts the sale
using that rate, and shows the draft for confirmation rather than asking for the rate again.

**3. Credit sale, new item never sold to this customer, rate genuinely unknown**
Owner: "Bilal ko 10 rawal plug likh do."
No matching item in Bilal's history or anywhere else in the shop's records — AI asks for the rate
explicitly rather than guessing a "typical" price.

**4. Partial payment against a running balance, explicit figure**
Owner: "Sharif ne 5000 de diye, poora nahi tha."
AI records a payment of exactly 5,000 against Sharif's balance — does not assume the balance is
now zero.

**5. Full settlement via "puri payment" wording, no number stated**
Owner: "Aslam ne apna poora hisaab kar diya."
AI resolves "poora hisaab" to Aslam's exact current outstanding balance server-side (never
estimates it) and drafts a full-balance payment for confirmation.

**6. Balance inquiry before extending more credit**
Owner: "Sharif ka kitna baaki hai?"
AI answers with Sharif's real current balance from the ledger — never from memory of the
conversation alone.

**7. Statement request for a monthly-settling dealer customer**
Owner: "Rasheed Hardware ko is mahine ka pura statement bhej do."
AI prepares a statement covering the current calendar month for that customer and queues it for
WhatsApp delivery once confirmed/connected.

**8. Invoice resend for the last bulk sale**
Owner: "Us contractor ko last invoice dobara bhej do."
AI resolves "last invoice" to that customer's most recent invoice-type document — never asks
which specific one, since the system always finds the latest automatically.

**9. Return of wrong-size fitting, same rate reversal**
Owner: "Wo elbow wapas aya, size ghalat tha, wapis kar do."
AI records a return of the specific elbow item at its original sale rate — not a new negotiated
rate, since this is a size-mismatch return, not a renegotiated price.

**10. Exchange rather than a straight return**
Owner: "Ali ne 1 inch pipe wapas kiya, adha inch de diya usko."
AI treats this as a return of one item plus a new sale of the replacement item, not a single
edited line, since the two items may have different rates.

**11. Quantity correction on today's entry**
Owner: "Sharif wali entry mein 5 nahi, 3 pipe the."
AI finds Sharif's specific matching entry from today and proposes an edit to the quantity (and
recalculated total) — asks which entry if more than one plausible match exists today.

**12. Price correction, not quantity**
Owner: "Wo rate galat likha, 250 nahi 220 tha."
AI edits the rate on the matched recent entry, recalculating the total — does not touch quantity.

**13. Discount given on a bulk order**
Owner: "Contractor ko 500 ka discount de diya total mein se."
AI reduces the recorded sale total by the stated discount amount, showing it as an explicit
adjustment on the draft rather than silently altering the item rates.

**14. Transport/delivery charge added separately**
Owner: "Maal bhej diya, 300 mazdoori alag se laga do Ali ke bill mein."
AI adds a separate transport/labor line item (not merged into product cost) per the "separate
transport charges" kind of rule an owner might also set as a Special Instruction.

**15. Advance payment before goods are delivered**
Owner: "Contractor ne 10,000 advance diye, maal baad mein jayega."
AI records a payment/credit against the customer now, with no matching sale entry yet — the sale
is drafted separately once goods actually go out.

**16. Multiple different products in one instruction**
Owner: "Ali ko 2 tap, 5 kilo kaeel, aur 3 kabza likh do, sab milakar 3200."
AI drafts a single sale with three line items whose combined total must equal 3,200 exactly — if
it doesn't reconcile, it asks rather than silently forcing the numbers to fit.

**17. Voice transcript with a self-correction**
Transcribed voice: "Sharif ko panch — nahi che — che sau ka pana becha."
AI treats "nahi" (no/not) as a correction marker and uses the corrected figure (600, "che sau"),
not the first-spoken one.

**18. Roman Urdu spelling variant of a date**
Owner: "20 tarikh ka hisaab batao."
AI resolves "tarikh" the same as "tareek"/"tareekh" — the 20th of the current (or most recently
past) month — and answers from the real ledger for that date.

**19. Mixed Urdu/English instruction**
Owner: "Please Sharif ka updated balance bhej do WhatsApp par."
AI treats this identically to an all-Roman-Urdu equivalent — sends (or prepares) Sharif's current
balance/statement, language-mixing changes nothing about intent recognition.

**20. Ambiguous item name requiring clarification**
Owner: "2 tape de do Ali ko."
This shop stocks both measuring tape and gum/sealant tape — with no disambiguating context, AI
asks which "tape" is meant rather than guessing.

**21. Follow-up question referring to "that" entry**
Owner: "Us wali entry ka date badal do, aaj ki nahi, kal ki thi."
AI uses the current active draft/most recent unconfirmed entry context to resolve "us wali" (that
one) rather than asking the owner to repeat which entry.

**22. Trade-specific edge case: negotiated bulk rate persisting across a job**
Owner: "Is contractor ko wahi rate dena jo pichli dafa tha, poore project ke liye."
AI notes the negotiated rate from the customer's recent history and applies it to the new item
being sold now, rather than reverting to the shop's general list rate.

**23. Trade-specific edge case: item sold by weight vs. by piece ambiguity**
Owner: "5 kaeel de do Sharif ko."
"5 kaeel" could mean 5 pieces or (unusually here) 5kg — given nails are conventionally sold by
weight in this trade, AI defaults its item-history lookup to Sharif's own past kaeel purchases to
resolve unit, asking only if his history has both.

**24. Recording an entry for a walk-in who becomes a named customer mid-conversation**
Owner: "Ek naya banda aya, naam Farhan, usko 3 tala de diye 600 ka, udhaar pe."
AI recognizes this needs a new customer record before the credit sale can be linked, and proposes
both — new customer creation followed by (or alongside) the sale draft — rather than recording an
untraceable udhaar entry.

**25. Owner asks what a customer bought on a specific date, multiple customers that day**
Owner: "8 tareekh ko kis kis ne kya liya?"
AI treats this as a whole-business period query for that single date, surfacing every customer's
entries from that day grouped by customer — not just the most recently discussed one.

**26. Correction after AI mismatches a customer name**
Owner: "Nahi wo Sharif nahi tha, Shahid tha."
AI treats this as an immediate correction to the just-created/just-proposed entry's customer link,
re-resolving to the real "Shahid" customer rather than creating a new "Shahid" record blindly if
an existing one is a clear match.

**27. Owner asks for total sales/collection for the day**
Owner: "Aaj ka total kitna hua?"
AI answers with today's real aggregate sales/collection figures from the ledger, not an estimate
carried over from earlier in the conversation.

**28. Dealer/wholesale monthly settlement with a stated payment method**
Owner: "Rasheed Hardware ne is mahine ka cheque se poora hisaab kar diya."
AI records a full-balance payment for that dealer with payment method "bank"/cheque as stated,
resolving the exact amount from the real current balance.

**29. Item quantity stated in a locally common but non-obvious phrase**
Owner: "Ek darjan kabza de do."
AI resolves "ek darjan" (one dozen) to 12 pieces of hinges, not a literal misreading as "1" unit.

**30. Owner references "usual" without stating what it means**
Owner: "Ali ko uske jaisa hamesha wala order de do."
Genuinely ambiguous with no clear single recent pattern in Ali's history — AI asks what "hamesha
wala" (the usual) refers to rather than guessing at a specific past order.

# Common AI Mistakes

Assuming a size/spec for a multi-variant item (pipe diameter, screw length) instead of asking or
matching real history. Treating a partial-payment phrase as a full settlement, or vice versa.
Confusing a physical return with an entry correction when the owner's wording is ambiguous
("wapas"/"wapis" can mean either). Silently applying the shop's average/list rate instead of the
specific customer's negotiated rate when one exists in history. Losing track of a mid-conversation
self-correction in a voice transcript and using the first-spoken (wrong) number.

# Best Practices

Always check the named customer's own purchase history first for item rate/spec resolution before
widening the search or asking. Keep multi-item single-instruction sales as one entry unless the
owner's own wording implies separate transactions. Never let a discount or transport charge get
silently folded into a product's per-unit rate — keep it as its own visible line/adjustment.
Prefer asking a short, specific clarifying question over guessing whenever a hardware-specific
ambiguity (size, unit, which "tape") is genuinely unresolved by history.
